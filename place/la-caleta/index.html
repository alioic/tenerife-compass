La Caleta

A former fishing hamlet at the quiet end of Costa Adeje, and the best place on the island to eat fish by the water.

Home› Places› South Tenerife› La Caleta

La Caleta sits at the northern edge of Costa Adeje, where the resorts run out and the coast turns rocky. It was a fishing village and still trades on it: a short seafront lined with fish restaurants, a small rocky bay, and a pace that drops noticeably the moment you arrive. There is no nightlife to speak of, which is exactly why people come.

What to see and do at La Caleta

Eating is the main event. The seafront restaurants serve the day's catch with papas arrugadas and mojo, and a table over the water at lunch, looking across to La Gomera, is one of the south's better hours. The village also holds several Michelin-starred kitchens in and around the Royal Hideaway Corales complex, including the two-starred El Rincón de Juan Carlos. Beyond the food, there is a small bay for a dip, a coastal path towards Playa del Duque, and not a great deal else.

How to get to La Caleta

About twenty-five minutes from Tenerife South Airport, at the end of the Costa Adeje seafront. You can walk along the promenade from Playa del Duque in roughly half an hour, or drive and park in the village, which gets tight at weekends and in the evening.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Book a table if you want a seafront spot at dinner, especially at weekends when locals drive down from inland. Lunch is the better value and the better light. Prices here are at the higher end for the island, and the bay is rocky rather than sandy, so bring water shoes if you plan to swim.

Good to know

La Caleta FAQ

Is La Caleta worth visiting?

For a meal, yes. It has the best concentration of seafront fish restaurants in the south and a far calmer atmosphere than the resorts. It is not a beach destination, as the bay is small and rocky.

Do you need to book restaurants in La Caleta?

For dinner and at weekends, yes. The seafront tables are the ones people want and they go early.
