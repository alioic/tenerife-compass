Puerto de la Cruz

Tenerife's original resort town, and still the one that feels most like a real Canarian place.

Home› Places› North Tenerife› Puerto de la Cruz

Puerto de la Cruz was welcoming visitors long before the southern resorts existed, and it wears that history well. It is a proper town rather than a strip: a working plaza, an old harbour, steep streets of Canarian houses, and a seafront built around the fact that the Atlantic here is often too rough to swim in.

What to see and do in Puerto de la Cruz

Lago Martiánez, the seawater lido complex designed by César Manrique, is the town's clever answer to that rough sea and a fine place to spend an afternoon. Playa Jardín, also landscaped by Manrique, is the main black-sand beach. Plaza del Charco is the social centre, ringed by cafés under old laurel trees, and the lanes around the old harbour repay an aimless wander. Loro Parque is a short shuttle ride away, and the botanical garden on the edge of town is one of the oldest in Spain.

How to get to Puerto de la Cruz

An hour and a quarter from the southern resorts by car on the TF-1 and TF-5, or on the direct TITSA 343 bus, which calls at both airports. From Santa Cruz it is around forty minutes. The town centre is walkable and parking is easier in the underground car parks than on the street.

Best time to go, and practical tips

This is the north, and the north has weather. Mornings can be cloudy when the south is clear, and it is a few degrees cooler year-round — pack a layer even in August. That trade-off buys you greenery, better food and lower prices than Costa Adeje. Carnival here in February is a serious event and accommodation books out well ahead.

Puerto de la Cruz FAQ

Is Puerto de la Cruz better than the south?

Different rather than better. It has more character, better food and lower prices, but cooler and cloudier weather and rougher sea. Sun-certainty favours the south; atmosphere favours the north.

Can you swim in Puerto de la Cruz?

Sometimes. Playa Jardín is an open Atlantic beach where the flags matter. The Lago Martiánez lido exists precisely for the days when the sea is rough.
