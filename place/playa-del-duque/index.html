Playa del Duque

The most upmarket stretch of sand in the south, and the one people picture when they picture Costa Adeje.

Home› Places› South Tenerife› Playa del Duque

Playa del Duque is where Costa Adeje puts on its best clothes. A Blue Flag crescent of golden sand backed by the Bahía del Duque resort and a run of designer shops, it is calmer and considerably smarter than the beaches further along the coast, and the water is usually gentle enough for a proper swim.

What to see and do at Playa del Duque

The beach itself is the attraction: soft imported sand, sun loungers and parasols for rent at roughly ten to fifteen euros, and a set of striped wooden beach houses that have become the postcard image of the resort. The promenade behind it runs north towards La Caleta and south towards Fañabé, so a stroll in either direction is easy. Plaza del Duque, a short walk inland, is the island's most upmarket shopping centre, and La Torre del Mirador sits above the sand for a long lunch with a view.

How to get to Playa del Duque

It is a twenty to twenty-five minute drive from Tenerife South Airport along the TF-1, and walkable from most of Costa Adeje along the seafront promenade. TITSA buses serve Costa Adeje from Los Cristianos and Playa de las Américas for a couple of euros, and parking near the beach fills early in peak season.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Mornings are noticeably quieter, and the light is better for photographs before the sun climbs. Sunbed rental is not cheap here compared with Los Cristianos, so bring a towel if you are counting euros. The UV is high year-round, even in winter, and the sea breeze hides how fast you are burning.

Good to know

Playa del Duque FAQ

Is Playa del Duque a good beach for families?

Yes. The sand is soft, the water is usually calm and there are lifeguards in season, plus plenty of facilities behind the beach. Playa de las Vistas in Los Cristianos is even more sheltered if you have very small children.

Is the sand at Playa del Duque natural?

Like several southern beaches, the golden sand is imported rather than the island's native volcanic black. It is one reason this stretch looks so different from the beaches of the north and west.

How much are sunbeds at Playa del Duque?

Expect roughly ten to fifteen euros for a lounger and parasol, which is at the higher end for the island. Prices vary by operator and season.
