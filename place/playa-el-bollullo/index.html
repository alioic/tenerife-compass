Playa El Bollullo

A black-sand cove below the banana terraces east of Puerto de la Cruz, and the north's prettiest beach.

Home› Places› North Tenerife› Playa El Bollullo

El Bollullo is a small cove of jet-black volcanic sand backed by cliffs and banana plantations, a few kilometres east of Puerto de la Cruz. The contrast of black sand against turquoise water makes it the most photographed beach in the north, and the short walk down keeps the crowds smaller than the numbers would suggest.

What to see and do at Playa El Bollullo

The beach, the cliffs and the water. There is a beach bar at the top of the path and little else, which is the appeal. Neighbouring coves — El Rincón and Los Patos — are reachable on foot along the shore at low tide, though this requires care and local advice about conditions. Surfers use this stretch when the swell is right.

How to get to Playa El Bollullo

Park at the end of the track from La Quinta, east of Puerto de la Cruz, and walk down a stepped path of five to ten minutes. The car park is small and fills at weekends. There is no direct bus, so a car or a taxi from Puerto de la Cruz is realistic.

Best time to go, and practical tips

This is an open Atlantic beach with real waves, currents and no lifeguard cover — swim with caution and get out if it is heavy. Black sand becomes genuinely hot underfoot in the middle of the day, so bring something for your feet. There is no shade, and the path back up is steeper than it looked on the way down.

Good to know

Playa El Bollullo FAQ

Can you swim at El Bollullo?

With caution. It is an open Atlantic beach with waves and currents and no lifeguards. Many visitors come for the setting and paddle rather than swim properly.

How do you get down to El Bollullo?

A stepped path of five to ten minutes from the small car park at the end of the track from La Quinta. There is no road access to the sand itself.
