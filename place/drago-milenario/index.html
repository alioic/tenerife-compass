Drago Milenario

The vast dragon tree of Icod, a symbol of the Canaries and probably around 800 years old.

Home› Places› North Tenerife› Drago Milenario

The Drago Milenario stands at the edge of Icod de los Vinos, an enormous dragon tree whose name claims a thousand years. Botanists put it closer to eight hundred, which is still remarkable for a species that leaves no growth rings to count. It is the emblem of the archipelago and the reason most visitors stop in Icod at all.

What to see and do at Drago Milenario

The tree is the sight. Paid entry to the Parque del Drago gets you close-up paths and a small botanical collection of Canarian species. Being honest, the free viewpoint beside the church square gives you a genuinely good view of the tree without the ticket, and many visitors are happy with that. The old town around Plaza de la Pila is pleasant for an hour, and Icod is a wine town, so a malvasía tasting fits naturally.

How to get to Drago Milenario

Roughly twenty minutes west of Puerto de la Cruz, or an hour and a half from the southern resorts. TITSA line 363 serves Icod along the north coast. There is paid parking near the park.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Decide in advance whether you want the ticket or the free view, as it changes how long you need from an hour to about fifteen minutes. Combine with Garachico, fifteen minutes further west, which most people find the more rewarding stop.

Good to know

Drago Milenario FAQ

Is the Drago Milenario really a thousand years old?

Probably not. The name means thousand-year dragon tree, but estimates put it closer to eight hundred years. Dragon trees produce no growth rings, so precise dating is impossible.

Do you have to pay to see the dragon tree?

No. Paid entry to Parque del Drago gets you closer and adds a small botanical garden, but there is a good free view from beside the church square.
