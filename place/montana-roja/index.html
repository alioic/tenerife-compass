Montaña Roja

A 171-metre red volcanic cone between two beaches, with the best coastal panorama in the south-east.

Home› Places› El Médano & the east› Montaña Roja

Montaña Roja is the red cone that anchors the coast between El Médano and La Tejita. It is protected as a special nature reserve for its dune habitat and birdlife, and a short, steep path climbs to the summit for a view along the whole south-eastern shoreline.

What to see and do at Montaña Roja

The climb is the thing: roughly thirty to forty minutes up over loose stones, with the reward of the coast laid out below and the kites of El Médano on one side, the long sweep of La Tejita on the other. Easier paths cross the reserve at the base and link the two beaches, which makes a pleasant circular walk of about two hours if you take in both.

How to get to Montaña Roja

Two free dirt car parks off the TF-643 between El Médano and Los Abrigos serve both the trailhead and La Tejita, with a five-minute walk over the dunes. From El Médano you can walk out along the coast instead.

Best time to go, and practical tips

There is no shade anywhere on the climb, so go early or late and take water. The stones are loose on the descent, so proper shoes help. Summer weekends fill the car parks. Combine it with a swim at La Tejita, remembering that the eastern end of that beach is a long-established naturist area.

Good to know

Montaña Roja FAQ

How hard is the Montaña Roja climb?

It is short but steep, roughly thirty to forty minutes up on loose stones. Most reasonably fit walkers manage it, but there is no shade at all.

Is La Tejita a nudist beach?

Partly. The eastern end nearest Montaña Roja is a long-established naturist area, while the rest of the beach is mixed.
