Vilaflor

The island's highest village, in the pine belt on the road up to Teide.

Home› Places› South Tenerife› Vilaflor

Vilaflor de Chasna sits at roughly 1,400 metres on the southern slope, in the Canary pine forest between the resorts and the national park. It is the highest village in Tenerife and the last real settlement before the road climbs into the crater. Cool air, pine scent and terraced hillsides make it feel a long way from the coast it looks down on.

What to see and do in Vilaflor

The village is small and takes an hour: a whitewashed church, steep lanes, terraces and viewpoints back down to the sea. What people really come for is what surrounds it. The Paisaje Lunar, the lunar landscape, is a set of pale eroded pumice pinnacles reached on a demanding walk of several hours, and it is one of the strangest sights on the island. Closer in, the Pino Gordo is among the largest Canary pines anywhere. Vilaflor is also the birthplace of Pedro de San José Betancur, born here in 1626 and canonised in 2002 as the first Canarian saint.

How to get to Vilaflor

About forty minutes up the TF-21 from Los Cristianos, on the main southern route to Teide, so it works naturally as a stop on the way up or down. TITSA line 482 links it with Los Cristianos. Parking in the village is limited to a few small areas.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Bring a layer. At this altitude it is several degrees cooler than the coast and evenings are genuinely cold, which is exactly why islanders come up here in August. The Paisaje Lunar walk is long, exposed and poorly shaded — take water, start early and do not attempt it casually. You will see Vilaflor described as the highest village in Spain; that claim is often repeated but contested, since villages in mainland Andalucía sit higher.

Good to know

Vilaflor FAQ

Is Vilaflor the highest village in Spain?

It is the highest in Tenerife, at around 1,400 metres. The frequently repeated claim that it is the highest in Spain is contested, as some mainland villages sit higher.

Is the Paisaje Lunar walk hard?

Yes. It is several hours, exposed, and climbs a good deal. Take plenty of water, start early and treat it as a proper hike rather than a stroll.

Is Vilaflor worth stopping at on the way to Teide?

Yes. It breaks the climb, it is the last village before the park, and the pine forest around it is a different island from the coast.
