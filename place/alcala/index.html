Alcalá

A small west-coast village built around a plaza and a natural sea pool.

Home› Places› West Tenerife› Alcalá

Alcalá is a few minutes north of Playa San Juan and smaller again: a plaza, a handful of streets, a little harbour and a natural seawater pool cut into the volcanic rock. Almost nothing here was built for tourism, and the village keeps the unhurried rhythm of somewhere that never expected visitors.

What to see and do in Alcalá

The natural pool is the reason to stop. Sheltered from the swell by the rock, it is swimmable on calm days and is where local families spend a Sunday. The plaza above it is the social centre, ringed by cafés, and there is a small beach and a coastal path linking the village with Playa San Juan. The Wednesday market is worth timing a visit around.

How to get to Alcalá

About thirty-five minutes north of Costa Adeje on the TF-47, five minutes beyond Playa San Juan. TITSA buses on the west coast route stop here. Parking near the plaza is easy except on market day.

Best time to go, and practical tips

The pool depends entirely on the sea state — excellent on a calm day, dangerous and best left alone when the swell is up, as anywhere on this coast. Alcalá has no resort infrastructure, so it is a half-day or an evening rather than a base for most visitors, though it makes a very peaceful one for anyone who wants nothing to happen.

Good to know

Alcalá FAQ

Can you swim in the natural pool at Alcalá?

On calm days, yes, and it is popular with local families. When the Atlantic swell is up it becomes dangerous and should be avoided.

Is Alcalá worth visiting?

If you want a Canarian village with no tourism built into it, yes. It is a half-day at most, and pairs naturally with Playa San Juan five minutes down the coast.
