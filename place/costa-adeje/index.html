Costa Adeje

The upmarket end of the southern coast: the best hotels, the smartest beaches and the highest prices.

Home› Places› South Tenerife› Costa Adeje

Costa Adeje is the purpose-built resort that runs north from Playa de las Américas to the old village of La Caleta. It holds most of the island's five-star hotels, a string of well-kept Blue Flag beaches and a promenade that ties it all together. It is comfortable rather than characterful, and it makes no apology for that.

What to see and do in Costa Adeje

The beaches step up in polish as you go north: Torviscas, then Fañabé, then Playa del Duque at the smart end. Siam Park sits above the resort and is the strongest single attraction in the south. Whale-watching boats leave from Puerto Colón marina. La Caleta at the northern edge has the best fish restaurants on this coast, and Plaza del Duque is the island's most upmarket shopping centre.

How to get to Costa Adeje

Twenty to twenty-five minutes from Tenerife South Airport on the TF-1. Taxis and pre-booked transfers are straightforward; the TITSA 111 also runs from the airport. Within the resort almost everything is walkable along the seafront.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Prices here are the island's highest, and the gap is real — the same dinner costs noticeably less in Los Cristianos twenty minutes south. The trade-off is quieter, better-maintained beaches and stronger hotels. For nightlife you will be heading to Las Américas; Costa Adeje goes quiet early, which for most visitors is the point.

Good to know

Costa Adeje FAQ

Is Costa Adeje better than Playa de las Américas?

For families, couples and anyone wanting quiet, yes — the beaches and hotels are better kept. Las Américas is cheaper and far livelier at night. They are a twenty-minute walk apart, so you can use both.

Is Costa Adeje expensive?

It is the most expensive part of the island for hotels and restaurants. Eating a street back from the seafront, or driving to Los Cristianos, cuts the bill significantly.
