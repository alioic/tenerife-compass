Taganana

One of the oldest settlements on the island, spilled down an Anaga valley towards the sea.

Home› Places› Santa Cruz & La Laguna› Taganana

Taganana sits in a steep green valley on the north side of the Anaga massif, white houses scattered among terraces of vines. It was settled very soon after the Spanish conquest and stayed effectively cut off for centuries — the road over the ridge is recent history. It remains one of the most striking approaches on the island.

What to see and do in Taganana

The village itself is a short wander: the church of Nuestra Señora de las Nieves, narrow lanes and terraced vineyards that still produce wine. What most people really come for is the coast just below — Roque de las Bodegas with its fish restaurants, then Almáciga, then Benijo at the end of the road. Walking trails climb from the village back up into the laurel forest.

How to get to Taganana

About forty-five minutes from Santa Cruz over the Anaga ridge on the TF-12 and TF-134, a beautiful and continuously winding drive through the laurel forest. Buses into Anaga are infrequent, so most visitors drive or take a guided tour. Parking in the village is very limited.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Expect cloud on the ridge on the way over even when the coast below is clear — that is how Anaga works. Combine the village with lunch at Roque de las Bodegas and the drive on to Benijo, which is the natural half-day. Anyone prone to car sickness should take the front seat and allow far longer than the distance suggests.

Good to know

Taganana FAQ

How do you get to Taganana?

By car over the Anaga ridge from Santa Cruz or La Laguna, around forty-five minutes on very winding mountain roads. Public transport into Anaga is sparse, so most visitors drive or join a tour.

Is there a beach at Taganana?

Not in the village itself, but the coast road below leads to Roque de las Bodegas, Almáciga and Benijo. These are wild Atlantic beaches with strong currents, better for looking than swimming.
