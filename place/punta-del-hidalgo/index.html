Punta del Hidalgo

Natural seawater pools at the end of the north coast road, where Anaga meets the Atlantic.

Home› Places› Santa Cruz & La Laguna› Punta del Hidalgo

Punta del Hidalgo is where the road along the north coast from La Laguna runs out, at the foot of the Anaga mountains. It is a village of locals rather than visitors, and its draw is a set of natural and part-built seawater pools along a rocky shore, plus some of the best walking on this side of the island starting from the edge of town.

What to see and do in Punta del Hidalgo

The rock pools are the main event and are free to use, sheltered enough to swim in on calm days. A strikingly modern concrete lighthouse stands at the point. The coastal path west to Bajamar is an easy walk of about an hour, and steeper trails climb from here up into Anaga towards Chinamada and its cave houses. There are unfussy local restaurants along the front.

How to get to Punta del Hidalgo

Around twenty-five minutes from La Laguna on the TF-13, or forty minutes from Santa Cruz. TITSA buses run from La Laguna. From the southern resorts, allow an hour and a quarter, or take the express coach to Santa Cruz and continue by tram and bus.

Best time to go, and practical tips

The pools depend entirely on the sea state. In swell they are dangerous and should be left alone; on a calm day they are excellent. This coast is cooler and cloudier than the south, so bring a layer. Sunday is when the village is at its most Canarian and its busiest.

Punta del Hidalgo FAQ

Can you swim at Punta del Hidalgo?

In the seawater pools, yes, on calm days — they are free and popular with locals. When the Atlantic swell is up they become dangerous and should be avoided.

Is Punta del Hidalgo worth visiting?

If you want the north coast without tourism, yes. It is a real village with free sea pools and excellent walking, and it pairs well with Bajamar or a hike into Anaga.
