Playa Jardín

Black volcanic sand backed by gardens designed by César Manrique, on the north coast.

Home› Places› North Tenerife› Playa Jardín

Playa Jardín is the main beach of Puerto de la Cruz: three linked bays of black volcanic sand, landscaped behind with subtropical gardens laid out by the Lanzarote artist César Manrique. It is the north's answer to the resort beaches of the south, and it looks nothing like them.

What to see and do at Playa Jardín

The gardens are as much of the attraction as the sand, full of palms and flowering shrubs with paths winding through. The beach faces the open Atlantic, so waves are part of the deal and surfers use it. Along the seafront you can walk to the old harbour and Plaza del Charco, and to the Lago Martiánez lido complex, also a Manrique design, which exists precisely because swimming in this sea is not always an option.

How to get to Playa Jardín

In Puerto de la Cruz, walkable from most of the town. From the southern resorts allow an hour and a quarter by car, or take the direct TITSA 343 bus, which runs a few times a day via both airports.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Watch the flags. This is a real Atlantic beach with genuine surf, and it behaves very differently from the sheltered southern bays. If the sea is rough, the Lago Martiánez lido nearby costs a few euros and solves the problem. The north is a few degrees cooler than the south with a real chance of morning cloud in any month.

Good to know

Playa Jardín FAQ

Can you swim at Playa Jardín?

Often yes, but this is an open Atlantic beach with real waves and currents. Follow the flags, and use the Lago Martiánez lido nearby when the sea is rough.

Why is the sand black at Playa Jardín?

It is the island's natural volcanic sand. The golden beaches of the south are mostly imported.
