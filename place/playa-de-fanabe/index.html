Playa de Fañabé

The big, busy family beach at the heart of Costa Adeje, with everything laid on behind it.

Home› Places› South Tenerife› Playa de Fañabé

Playa de Fañabé is the long golden beach in the middle of Costa Adeje, and the one most package holidays end up walking down to. It is Blue Flag, well serviced and backed by a solid wall of hotels, which tells you both what is good about it and what is not: everything you need is thirty seconds away, and you will not have it to yourself.

What to see and do at Playa de Fañabé

Swimming, sunbathing and watersports, mostly. The sand is imported and golden, the water is usually calm enough for children, and there are lifeguards in season. Parasailing, jet skis and banana boats operate off the beach through the summer. The promenade behind runs north to Playa del Duque and south towards Playa de las Américas, and Puerto Colón marina, five minutes away, is where the whale-watching and catamaran trips leave from.

How to get to Playa de Fañabé

About twenty to twenty-five minutes from Tenerife South Airport on the TF-1, and a flat walk from most of Costa Adeje. TITSA buses serve the resort from Los Cristianos and Playa de las Américas. Street parking near the beach is scarce in high season; the hotel car parks are the realistic option.

Best time to go, and practical tips

The middle of the beach is the busiest part, and it thins out noticeably at either end. Sunbeds cost a little less here than at Playa del Duque next door. Prices at the seafront restaurants reflect their position, so walk one street back if you are eating more than once. Afternoon breeze picks up along this whole coast, which is welcome on the sand and less so if you are trying to read.

Good to know

Playa de Fañabé FAQ

Is Playa de Fañabé good for families?

Yes. The water is usually calm, there are lifeguards in season, and the facilities behind the beach are excellent. It is busier than Playa del Duque and cheaper for sunbeds.

Is the sand at Fañabé natural?

No. Like most of the golden beaches in the south, the sand was brought in. The island's natural sand is the black volcanic kind you see in the north and west.
