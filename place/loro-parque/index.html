Loro Parque

The north's biggest attraction: a zoo and botanical park that began as a parrot collection in 1972.

Home› Places› North Tenerife› Loro Parque

Loro Parque opened in Puerto de la Cruz in 1972 with a few dozen parrots and has grown into the north's flagship attraction, part zoo and part botanical garden, set in dense subtropical planting. The parrot collection remains one of the largest in the world and is what the name still refers to.

What to see and do at Loro Parque

Beyond the parrots there are penguins in a refrigerated hall, gorillas, big cats, an aquarium with a shark tunnel, and animal presentations through the day. The planting is a genuine attraction in itself. Allow the best part of a day. Worth knowing before you book: like other parks that keep marine mammals, Loro Parque draws criticism from animal welfare campaigners, and its cetacean collection has changed in recent years — check the park's own site for what is currently on show rather than relying on older guides.

How to get to Loro Parque

In Puerto de la Cruz, with a free vintage-style train shuttling from the town centre. From the southern resorts allow an hour and a quarter by car, or take the direct TITSA 343 bus. Twin tickets combining Loro Parque with Siam Park in the south are sold at both parks and by tour operators, and work out cheaper than two separate day tickets.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Go early, both for the animals and for the queues. The north is cooler and cloudier than the south, so a jacket is sensible even in summer, and the penguin house is deliberately cold. If you are staying in the south, the twin ticket only pays off if you will genuinely use both parks, since they are an hour and a half apart.

Loro Parque FAQ

Is Loro Parque worth visiting?

For most visitors to the north, yes — it is the biggest attraction on that coast and the botanical setting is genuinely good. Visitors uncomfortable with marine mammals in captivity should check the park's current animal collection before booking.

Is the twin ticket with Siam Park worth it?

Only if you will use both. The two parks are an hour and a half apart on opposite coasts, so it means two separate day trips, but the combined price is well below two single tickets.
