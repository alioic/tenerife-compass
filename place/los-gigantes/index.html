Los Gigantes

Sheer cliffs up to about 500 metres falling straight into the Atlantic, best seen from the water.

Home› Places› West Tenerife› Los Gigantes

The Acantilados de los Gigantes, the Cliffs of the Giants, are the most imposing piece of coastline in Tenerife. The resort town of the same name sits at their foot, built on steep streets that tumble down to a small marina tucked right against the cliff wall.

What to see and do at Los Gigantes

The cliffs are the attraction, and they are far more impressive from the sea than from land. Boat trips leave the marina for two to three hours beneath the rock faces, usually with a swim stop at Masca bay, and pilot whales and dolphins are commonly seen on the way. Kayak and snorkel trips run along the same coast. On land, the Mirador de Archipenque above town gives the classic view, and Playa de los Guíos is a small black-sand beach beside the marina.

How to get to Los Gigantes

Roughly twenty-five to thirty-five minutes from Costa Adeje via the TF-1 and TF-47, and about forty-five minutes from Tenerife South Airport. Parking in the town is genuinely tight, so use the car parks above and walk down.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Morning boat trips usually have calmer water. The whole coast faces west, which makes this one of the best places on the island for sunset, either from the Mirador de Archipenque or from an evening sailing. Treat the Crab Island tidal pool north of the marina with real caution and obey closures, as there have been fatalities there.

Good to know

Los Gigantes FAQ

What is the best way to see the Los Gigantes cliffs?

From the water. Boat trips from the marina run for two to three hours from around thirty euros and often include a swim stop and dolphin sightings.

Is Los Gigantes a good base for a holiday?

It suits couples, walkers and anyone wanting quiet with dramatic scenery. There is no nightlife strip, and beaches here are small and black-sand.
