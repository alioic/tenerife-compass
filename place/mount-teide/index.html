Mount Teide

Spain's highest peak at 3,715 metres, and the reason the island looks the way it does.

Home› Places› Teide National Park› Mount Teide

Teide rises 3,715 metres out of the Atlantic, which makes it the highest point in Spain and, measured from the ocean floor, one of the tallest volcanic structures on Earth. The national park around it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2007 and is among the most visited national parks anywhere. Even if you never leave the coast, the mountain is on your horizon most days.

What to see and do on Mount Teide

Most visitors take the cable car from the base station up to around 3,555 metres, an eight-minute ride that leaves a short walk to two viewpoints. Going higher, onto the Telesforo Bravo path to the actual crater rim, needs a free permit booked in advance through the national parks website, and the daily quota goes early. Lower down, the crater floor is arguably the better experience: the Roques de García rock formations, the Llano de Ucanca plain and a landscape so lunar that film crews use it. After dark the park is one of the world's better stargazing sites, with organised night tours from the resorts.

How to get to Mount Teide

About an hour and a half by car from the southern resorts on the TF-21 via Vilaflor, or from the north via La Orotava. Two TITSA buses serve the park daily: the 342 from Costa Adeje and the 348 from Puerto de la Cruz, both leaving in the morning and returning mid-afternoon. Many visitors book a guided tour instead, which removes the mountain driving and usually includes the cable car.

Best time to go, and practical tips

It is genuinely cold up there. The summit can sit below freezing while the coast is in shorts weather, and the wind makes it worse, so bring layers whatever the forecast says at sea level. The cable car closes in high wind, which happens often enough to be worth checking before you drive up. Altitude affects some people at 3,555 metres; take it slowly, and anyone with heart or breathing problems should think carefully. Book the summit permit and the cable car well ahead in peak season.

Mount Teide FAQ

Do you need a permit to climb Mount Teide?

You need a free permit only for the Telesforo Bravo path above the cable car station to the actual summit. It is booked in advance through the national parks reservation website and the daily quota fills early. Everything below that, including the cable car and the crater floor, is open to all.

How cold is it at the top of Teide?

Much colder than the coast — often near or below freezing at the top station, and windier. Take proper layers even on a hot day at sea level.

Can you get to Teide by bus?

Yes. TITSA line 342 runs from Costa Adeje and 348 from Puerto de la Cruz, once each per day, up in the morning and back in the afternoon. It works, but it gives you a fixed and fairly short window in the park.
