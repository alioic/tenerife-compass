Garachico

The north's prettiest small town, with natural rock pools made by the lava that destroyed its harbour.

Home› Places› North Tenerife› Garachico

Garachico was the richest port in Tenerife until a volcanic eruption in 1706 poured lava through the town and filled the harbour. What was a catastrophe left behind El Caletón, a set of natural pools in the cooled lava that people now swim in, and a compact old town of cobbled lanes, convents and plazas that many visitors rate their favourite on the island.

What to see and do at Garachico

The rock pools are the draw: a series of channels and basins in the black lava, swimmable on calm days and closed when the swell is up. The old town behind them rewards an hour of wandering, and there are two of the island's loveliest historic hotels here. It is a half day at minimum, and genuinely lovely to stay overnight once the day-trippers have gone.

How to get to Garachico

About an hour and a half from the southern resorts, or twenty-five minutes west of Puerto de la Cruz along the north coast. TITSA line 363 runs along this coast from Puerto de la Cruz. There are free car parks on the seafront by the pools.

Best time to go, and practical tips

The pools are conditions-dependent and are at their best roughly May to October; in heavy swell they are dangerous and closed, and that should be respected. The north is cooler and cloudier than the south, so bring a layer. Combine it with Icod de los Vinos and its dragon tree, fifteen minutes away.

Good to know

Garachico FAQ

Can you swim in the rock pools at Garachico?

Yes, on calm days, and they are one of the most enjoyable swims in the north. They are closed and genuinely dangerous when the Atlantic swell is up, so check conditions and obey any closures.

Is Garachico worth visiting?

It is many visitors' favourite town on the island: compact, historic and beautiful, with the rock pools as a bonus. Half a day minimum, and better as an overnight stop.
