San Cristóbal de La Laguna

Tenerife's original capital, a UNESCO World Heritage grid whose plan was copied across colonial Latin America.

Home› Places› Santa Cruz & La Laguna› San Cristóbal de La Laguna

La Laguna was the island's first capital and is now a lively university town, listed by UNESCO since 1999 because its unfortified sixteenth-century grid became the model for Spanish colonial cities in the Americas. The old centre is pastel mansions, carved wooden balconies and pedestrian streets full of students, and it has the best-value tapas on the island.

What to see and do at San Cristóbal de La Laguna

Wander first: Calle Obispo Rey Redondo and Calle Herradores are the spine, lined with cafés and tapas bars. Climb the tower of the Iglesia de la Concepción for a couple of euros and the best view over the grid of roofs. Plaza del Adelantado anchors the old town, and the cathedral and the historic mansions fill the streets between. In the evening the tapas bars are the point.

How to get to San Cristóbal de La Laguna

Tram line 1 links Santa Cruz with La Laguna in about thirty to forty minutes for well under two euros, which is by far the easiest way. From the southern resorts, take the 110 or 111 express bus to Santa Cruz and change to the tram, or drive in about an hour.

Best time to go, and practical tips

At around 550 metres the town is several degrees cooler than the coast, and locals call it the fridge of Tenerife, so bring a layer even in summer. The old town is pedestrianised, so park on the edge. Pair it with Anaga, which begins just above the town.

San Cristóbal de La Laguna FAQ

Why is La Laguna a UNESCO site?

Its unfortified grid plan from the late fifteenth and sixteenth centuries became the template for Spanish colonial towns across the Americas, and the historic centre survives largely intact.

How do you get to La Laguna from Santa Cruz?

Tram line 1 runs between the two in roughly thirty to forty minutes for a couple of euros, and it is much easier than driving and parking.
