La Orotava

Steep cobbled streets and carved Canarian balconies, on the hillside above Puerto de la Cruz.

Home› Places› North Tenerife› La Orotava

La Orotava sits above Puerto de la Cruz on the slope running down from Teide, and it is the best-preserved historic town in the north. Wealth from sugar and wine built it, and the money shows: carved wooden balconies, painted mansions and a hillside grid of cobbled streets steep enough to make you notice.

What to see and do in La Orotava

The Casa de los Balcones is the landmark, a seventeenth-century house whose galleried Canarian pine balconies appear on half the postcards of the island. The Iglesia de la Concepción has one of the finest baroque façades in the Canaries, and the Victoria Gardens step up the hillside behind. At Corpus Christi the town lays enormous carpets of coloured volcanic sand across the square in front of the town hall, which is worth planning a trip around.

How to get to La Orotava

Ten minutes uphill from Puerto de la Cruz, or about forty minutes from Santa Cruz. From the southern resorts allow an hour and a quarter. The TF-21 continues up from here towards Teide, so it works well as a stop on the way. Park at the edge and walk in; the old streets are narrow and steep.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Wear shoes you can walk uphill in — the gradient is real. Many shops still close for a long lunch. Combine it with Puerto de la Cruz below or with a drive up to Teide above, since it sits on the road between the two.

Good to know

La Orotava FAQ

Is La Orotava worth visiting?

Yes, particularly if you like historic architecture. It is the best-preserved old town in the north and takes a couple of hours, easily combined with Puerto de la Cruz or a trip up to Teide.

When are the sand carpets in La Orotava?

They are laid for Corpus Christi, which falls in late spring or early summer depending on the year. Check the local dates before planning around it.
