Santa Cruz de Tenerife

The island's capital: a working Spanish city with a Calatrava landmark, a famous market and the world's second-largest carnival.

Home› Places› Santa Cruz & La Laguna› Santa Cruz de Tenerife

Santa Cruz is where the island actually works. It is a port city rather than a resort, with department stores, office workers and a waterfront that has been rebuilt around Santiago Calatrava's Auditorio. Visitors who only see the south often skip it, which is a mistake if you want any sense of what Tenerife is like when it is not selling itself.

What to see and do in Santa Cruz de Tenerife

The Auditorio is the landmark, its great white wave visible from the motorway. Plaza de España is built around a shallow artificial lake designed by Herzog & de Meuron. The Mercado de Nuestra Señora de África is the best market on the island for produce, cheese and fish. Add the TEA arts centre, the palm-filled Parque García Sanabria and Calle del Castillo for shopping. In February the city hosts a carnival that is generally reckoned second only to Rio.

How to get to Santa Cruz de Tenerife

About an hour from the southern resorts on the TF-1, and much easier by the TITSA 110 or 111 express coaches, which run frequently and drop you at the intercambiador in the centre. From there tram line 1 continues to La Laguna. Driving in is fine; parking is easiest in the underground car parks near the Auditorio.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Most shops still close for a long lunch and stay shut on Sunday, so plan around that. The market is a morning place. Combine the city with La Laguna, thirty minutes away by tram, and you have a full and genuinely interesting day. If you are here in February, book accommodation months ahead.

Santa Cruz de Tenerife FAQ

Is Santa Cruz worth a day trip?

Yes, particularly combined with La Laguna up the tram line. It is a real city rather than a resort, with the island's best market and shopping and a genuinely striking waterfront.

How do you get to Santa Cruz from the south?

The TITSA 110 and 111 express coaches run frequently from Los Cristianos and Costa Adeje and take around an hour, which is easier than driving and parking.
