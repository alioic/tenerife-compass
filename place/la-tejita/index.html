Playa de la Tejita

One of the longest natural beaches on the island, undeveloped, with Montaña Roja at one end.

Home› Places› El Médano & the east› Playa de la Tejita

La Tejita runs for roughly a kilometre of natural golden sand between Montaña Roja and El Médano, and it is one of the few big beaches in the south with almost nothing built on it. That emptiness is the appeal, and it comes with the trade-offs you would expect: little shade, few facilities and an exposed Atlantic shoreline.

What to see and do at Playa de la Tejita

The beach and the volcano behind it are the whole attraction. The path up Montaña Roja starts at the eastern end and gives the best coastal view in this corner of the island. There is a beach bar at the western end in season. The eastern end, nearest the volcano, is a long-established naturist area; the rest is mixed and nobody makes a fuss either way.

How to get to Playa de la Tejita

Two free dirt car parks off the TF-643 between El Médano and Los Abrigos put you five minutes from the sand across the dunes. It is around ten minutes from Tenerife South Airport, or a walk of about half an hour along the coast from El Médano.

Best time to go, and practical tips

There is no natural shade, so bring an umbrella if you plan to stay. The same trade winds that make El Médano a windsurfing centre blow here, strongest between May and September — superb if you like a wild beach, wearing if you want to read a book. Swim with care: this is open Atlantic with no lifeguard cover along most of its length.

Good to know

Playa de la Tejita FAQ

Is La Tejita a nudist beach?

Partly. The eastern end nearest Montaña Roja is a long-established naturist area. The rest of the beach is mixed and textile.

Is La Tejita windy?

Often, yes. It sits on the same stretch of coast as El Médano, where the trade winds are strongest from May to September. Autumn and early winter are calmer.
