Playa de Benijo

Black sand, jagged rock stacks and serious Atlantic surf at the end of the Anaga road.

Home› Places› Santa Cruz & La Laguna› Playa de Benijo

Playa de Benijo sits at the far end of the Anaga coast road, where the mountains fall straight into the ocean. Black volcanic sand, offshore rock stacks and heavy surf make it the most photographed beach on the island, and one of the least suitable for a casual swim.

What to see and do at Playa de Benijo

The view is the reason to come, particularly late in the day when the sun drops behind the rocks. A stepped path leads down from the road to the sand. Walkers can continue along the coast towards El Draguillo, and the restaurants above the beach serve fresh fish with one of the better dining views in Tenerife. At low tide the beach opens out considerably.

How to get to Playa de Benijo

About an hour from Santa Cruz on the TF-12 and TF-134 through Anaga, a spectacular but relentlessly winding drive. There is no easy bus, so a car or an organised tour is realistic. Parking at the end of the road is very limited and fills at weekends.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Swim with great caution or not at all: the Atlantic here has strong currents and there are no lifeguards. Go on a weekday if you can, and allow far longer for the drive than the distance suggests. Anyone prone to car sickness should sit in the front. Mist on the ridge is normal even when the coast is sunny.

Good to know

Playa de Benijo FAQ

Can you swim at Playa de Benijo?

Only with great caution. The surf is strong, there are currents and no lifeguards. Most visitors come for the view and the walk rather than to swim.

How do you get to Benijo without a car?

Buses into Anaga are sparse and slow. Most visitors drive or join a guided Anaga tour from the south or from Santa Cruz.
