Masca

A hamlet clinging to a ridge deep in the Teno mountains, at the head of Tenerife's most dramatic gorge.

Home› Places› West Tenerife› Masca

Masca is a scatter of houses on a knife-edge ridge, surrounded by ravines that drop towards the sea. It is the island's most photographed village and the starting point of the Barranco de Masca hike, and getting there is half the story: the road in is one of the most demanding scenic drives in Europe.

What to see and do at Masca

The village itself takes an hour: a church, a few terraces, several cafés with staggering views, and paths that lead a short way out onto the ridge for the classic photograph. The serious attraction is the gorge below, a steep one-way descent to a hidden cove, which is now strictly regulated and must be booked in advance. Most visitors simply come for the drive, the view and lunch.

How to get to Masca

The TF-436 from Santiago del Teide is about twenty minutes and roughly five kilometres of hairpins, barely two cars wide in places, with steep drops and coaches coming the other way. Confident drivers only, and go early. Parking in the village is very limited. Hikers doing the gorge must arrive on the TITSA 355 bus, as private cars are not permitted for them.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Early morning and late afternoon are both quieter and better lit than the middle of the day, when tour coaches arrive. If the drive sounds like too much, an organised tour or a boat trip from Los Gigantes to Masca bay gives you the scenery without the hairpins.

Good to know

Masca FAQ

Is the road to Masca dangerous?

It is narrow and full of hairpins with steep drops, and it demands confidence and full attention, but it is well surfaced. Go early, drive slowly and expect to meet coaches.

Do you need to book to visit Masca village?

No, the village is free to visit. Only the gorge hike below it requires a paid permit and arrival by the official bus.
