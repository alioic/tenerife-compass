Playa de las Teresitas

A golden crescent of Saharan sand a short drive from the capital, and arguably the prettiest big beach on the island.

Home› Places› Santa Cruz & La Laguna› Playa de las Teresitas

Playa de las Teresitas curves for over a kilometre beside the fishing village of San Andrés, north-east of Santa Cruz. The golden sand was shipped in from the Sahara in the early 1970s and a breakwater keeps the water flat, which makes it both beautiful and unusually calm for this coast. What it is not is a resort: locals outnumber tourists most weekends, and that is its charm.

What to see and do at Playa de las Teresitas

Swimming and lying still, mostly. Palms line the back of the beach, there are showers and beach bars, and parking behind the sand is free. San Andrés at the near end is a working fishing village with a run of good fish restaurants, and the road onwards leads into the Anaga mountains, so the beach pairs naturally with a drive into the hills.

How to get to Playa de las Teresitas

Roughly fifteen minutes north-east of Santa Cruz by car or local bus. From the southern resorts allow around an hour on the TF-1, or take the 110 or 111 express bus to Santa Cruz and continue locally.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Sunny weekends fill it with local families, which is part of the experience but means arriving early for a parking space. It is noticeably more sheltered than the wild Anaga beaches further round the coast, so it suits families where Benijo does not. Combine it with lunch in San Andrés and an afternoon in Anaga.

Good to know

Playa de las Teresitas FAQ

Is the sand at Las Teresitas imported?

Yes. The golden sand was brought from the Sahara in the early 1970s. The island's own beaches on this coast are volcanic black.

Can you swim at Playa de las Teresitas?

Yes, and it is one of the calmer big beaches on the island thanks to the breakwater that shelters it.
