Pirámides de Güímar

Six stepped platforms of lava stone, a Norwegian explorer, and an argument that is still going.

Home› Places› El Médano & the east› Pirámides de Güímar

On the edge of Güímar stand six rectangular stepped structures built from lava stone. Thor Heyerdahl, the Norwegian who sailed the Kon-Tiki, moved to Tenerife in the 1990s, became convinced they were ancient, and set up the ethnographic park that protects them today. Mainstream archaeology is far less convinced, and that unresolved argument is much of what makes the visit interesting.

What to see and do at Pirámides de Güímar

The pyramids themselves, plus a well-made museum covering Heyerdahl's expeditions, a replica of the Ra II reed boat, and gardens planted with species from the places his voyages touched. The park sets out the competing explanations fairly: Heyerdahl's transatlantic theory on one hand, and the widely held view that the structures are nineteenth-century terracing built from stones cleared off farmland on the other. Allow around two hours.

How to get to Pirámides de Güímar

Roughly thirty minutes south of Santa Cruz on the TF-1, or about forty-five minutes from the southern resorts. There is parking at the park. Buses run to Güímar from Santa Cruz, with a short walk or taxi at the far end.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Go for the ideas as much as the stones — visitors expecting Egypt are usually disappointed, while visitors who enjoy an unresolved argument tend to leave happy. Much of the site is outdoors with limited shade. It combines neatly with Candelaria, twenty minutes north up the coast.

Pirámides de Güímar FAQ

Are the Güímar pyramids really ancient?

That is disputed. Thor Heyerdahl argued they were, and built a case around transatlantic contact. The prevailing archaeological view is that they are nineteenth-century agricultural terracing made from field stones. The park presents both.

Is it worth visiting the Pirámides de Güímar?

If you enjoy the argument and the Heyerdahl story, yes — the museum is good and it takes about two hours. Visitors expecting monumental ancient architecture usually find it underwhelming.
