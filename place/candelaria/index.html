Candelaria

The spiritual capital of the Canary Islands, with nine bronze Guanche kings facing the sea.

Home› Places› El Médano & the east› Candelaria

Candelaria, on the east coast south of Santa Cruz, is where Canarians come rather than where tour buses stop. Its basilica holds the image of the Virgin of Candelaria, patron saint of the whole archipelago, and the square in front of it is guarded by nine bronze statues of Guanche kings looking out at the Atlantic.

What to see and do in Candelaria

The Basílica de Nuestra Señora de la Candelaria dominates the seafront square, and the statues of the nine menceyes — the chiefs who ruled the island before the Spanish conquest — are the photograph everyone takes. Beyond that it is a pleasant coastal town with black-sand beaches, a promenade and unpretentious fish restaurants. The big pilgrimage falls in mid-August and draws people on foot from across the island.

How to get to Candelaria

Around twenty minutes south of Santa Cruz on the TF-1, or roughly forty-five minutes from the southern resorts. TITSA buses run along this coast from Santa Cruz. There is parking near the seafront, which fills on Sundays and during festivals.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Sunday is when the town is at its most alive and its most crowded. The beaches here are black volcanic sand and the sea is more exposed than the sheltered southern bays, so treat swimming with the usual Atlantic caution. It pairs well with the Güímar pyramids, twenty minutes further south.

Good to know

Candelaria FAQ

Who are the statues at Candelaria?

They are the nine menceyes, the Guanche chiefs who ruled the districts of Tenerife before the Spanish conquest. The bronzes stand along the seafront in front of the basilica.

Is Candelaria worth visiting?

If you want a side of the island that is not built for tourism, yes. It is the religious heart of the Canaries and a genuine Canarian town, best combined with the Güímar pyramids nearby.
