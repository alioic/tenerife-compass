El Médano

A low-rise surf town wrapped around a sandy bay, and one of Europe's best-known wind-sports spots.

Home› Places› El Médano & the east› El Médano

El Médano is what the south of Tenerife looks like without the resorts: a real Canarian town on a wide sandy bay, ten minutes from the airport, where the constant trade winds have built one of Europe's great windsurfing and kitesurfing scenes. Board shops, surf cafés and a wooden boardwalk set the tone.

What to see and do at El Médano

The bay fills with sails on most summer days and the kite beach at El Cabezo, just east of town, is where the serious riders go. Schools run lessons year-round for beginners. The boardwalk links the plaza, the cafés and the beaches, and the red cone of Montaña Roja rises at the far end with an easy path to the summit and the best coastal view in the area. La Tejita, a long natural beach with a naturist section, sits just beyond it.

How to get to El Médano

About ten minutes from Tenerife South Airport, which makes it the easiest arrival on the island. TITSA line 470 links it with Los Abrigos, Golf del Sur, Las Galletas and Los Cristianos. Town parking is tight on Wednesday and Saturday market mornings.

Best time to go, and practical tips

Be honest with yourself about wind. It is most reliable from May to September and peaks in July and August, which is superb for sport and genuinely annoying for lying on a towel. Autumn and early winter bring calmer days. Playa Chica is the sheltered corner when it blows.

Good to know

El Médano FAQ

Is El Médano too windy for a beach holiday?

It can be. The trade winds are strongest from May to September. If you want still days on the sand, choose the south-west resorts or visit in autumn.

Can beginners learn to kitesurf in El Médano?

Yes. Several schools run lessons year-round, with tasters from around a hundred and twenty euros and longer beginner courses over several days.
