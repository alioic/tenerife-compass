Do you use euros in Tenerife?

Home› Questions› Money

Short answer

Yes. Tenerife is part of Spain and the European Union and uses the euro. The wrinkle worth knowing is tax: the Canary Islands sit outside the EU VAT area and apply their own lower local tax instead, which is why several things are cheaper here — and why duty-free rules apply on the way home.

Euros, cards and cash

The euro has been the currency since 2002 and nothing about paying here is unusual. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, contactless is standard, and ATMs are plentiful in the resorts and towns. Small village bars, market stalls and some taxi drivers still prefer cash, so carrying a modest amount is sensible once you leave the resort strip.

One thing to watch at ATMs and card terminals: if you are offered the choice of being charged in your home currency rather than euros, decline it. That is dynamic currency conversion, and the exchange rate applied is almost always worse than your own bank's. Always choose to pay in euros.

The tax quirk that actually affects you

The Canary Islands are politically part of Spain and the EU, but they are excluded from the EU VAT area for historical and geographical reasons. Instead of Spanish VAT they levy IGIC, the Canarian general indirect tax, at rates well below mainland VAT.

That is the real explanation behind something visitors notice immediately: alcohol, tobacco, perfume, cosmetics and electronics are cheaper here than on the mainland or in northern Europe. It is not a duty-free gimmick aimed at tourists — it is simply the local tax system, and residents benefit from it too.

Duty-free on the way home

The same exclusion has a consequence at the airport. Because the Canaries sit outside the EU VAT and excise area, travelling from Tenerife to mainland Spain or to another EU country counts as leaving that area for duty purposes. Duty-free shopping exists at Tenerife's airports for that reason, and personal allowances apply to what you carry home.

Those allowances are set by your destination country and change from time to time, so check the current limits with your own customs authority rather than relying on a guide — including this one. What is worth knowing in advance is simply that a limit exists, because people do get caught out assuming an EU-to-EU flight has none.

  • Always decline conversion to your home currency
  • IGIC, not VAT — which is why some things are cheaper
  • Duty-free allowances apply even flying to the EU

Related questions

Is Tenerife in the EU?

Yes. The Canary Islands are part of Spain and the European Union, though they are excluded from the EU VAT area and have their own lower indirect tax.

Why is alcohol cheaper in Tenerife?

Because the Canaries apply IGIC rather than mainland Spanish VAT, at considerably lower rates. It applies to residents as well as visitors.

Do you need cash in Tenerife?

Not much. Cards work almost everywhere, but small bars, market stalls and some taxis prefer cash, so carry a little.

Is there duty free flying from Tenerife to the UK or EU?

Yes. Because the Canaries sit outside the EU VAT and excise area, duty-free applies. Allowances are set by your destination country and change, so check current limits before buying.
