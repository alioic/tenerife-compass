The Masca gorge hike

A steep, one-way descent through a five-kilometre ravine to a cove on the west coast, and the only way to walk it is with a paid permit booked in advance on the official site.

Home› Masca gorge hike

Before you plan anything

You cannot simply turn up and walk. Three things must line up: a paid permit with a timed slot, booked online; arrival on the official bus, because private cars may not drop hikers at the trailhead; and a boat ticket out of Masca bay, bought separately. Miss one and you are turned away at the visitor centre. There is no walking back up the gorge.

The rules here have been rewritten more than once since the trail reopened, most recently on how you are allowed to reach the start. Everything below is what the official operator publishes at the time of writing, but verify prices, timings and closures on caminobarrancodemasca.com before you build a day around it.

The route

What the hike actually is

The Barranco de Masca is a ravine that starts just below the mountain hamlet of Masca and drops to a small cove on the Atlantic. You walk down it, not along it. The path crosses and recrosses the stream bed, squeezes between rock walls that close in overhead, and passes pools and palms in the shaded stretches, then narrows sharply before opening onto the beach where the boat picks you up.

It is about five kilometres and takes roughly four hours, which tells you most of what you need to know. Nobody spends four hours covering five kilometres on an easy path. The ground is loose rock and uneven stone, there are short scrambles, and the descent is relentless on the knees. In return you get a landscape that feels genuinely remote for an island this developed. The permit covers a timed slot, a helmet on loan, ranger supervision, first aid and rescue cover, and accident insurance. Rockfall is why the helmet is compulsory.

Logistics

How to book, step by step

  1. Book the trail permit online at caminobarrancodemasca.com and pick a date and entry time. Slots are capped and popular dates sell out, so book well ahead.
  2. Book the boat out of Masca bay. A separate purchase from a partner operator, not included in the permit. Choose a departure comfortably after your slot so a slow descent does not strand you.
  3. Get yourself to Santiago del Teide by car or public bus, then take the TITSA 355 shuttle up to the trailhead. Hikers arriving by private car are refused, so park in the village.
  4. Be at the bus stop early. Roughly an hour before your slot at the departure point, and at the Masca visitor centre check-in about half an hour before. The ride up is around twenty minutes.
  5. Bring ID and the right kit. Permits are personal and non-transferable, so carry photo ID matching the booking, plus your bus proof and boat ticket. Boots are checked at the entrance.
  6. Plan the way back from Los Gigantes. The boat lands you there, not at your car. Public buses run to Santiago del Teide, but check the timetable before you set off, not after you land.

Straight talk

How hard is it, honestly

Hard enough that the operator screens your boots at the door. This is a mountain route, not a scenic walk with a café halfway. Four hours of continuous downhill on loose rock is punishing on knees and ankles, heat builds in the lower gorge where there is no breeze, and once you are an hour in the only ways out are forward to the beach or a rescue.

Regular hikers find it tiring but wonderful. People who last walked any distance a year ago find the final hour miserable. If you have knee trouble, poor balance, a fear of exposure, or a child who would be marched along rather than enjoying themselves, this is the wrong day out.

If you do go: carry more water than feels sensible, start early for the shade, eat beforehand, and use poles if you own them. Leave slack between your entry slot and your boat, because the gorge takes longer than people plan for.

Alternatives

If it is not for you

Plenty of people come to Tenerife wanting Masca and leave happy without setting foot in the gorge. The village itself is free to visit: stone houses strung along a knife-edge ridge, a couple of cafés, terraced gardens, and views that are hard to overstate. It fills up at midday, so go early or stay for the late light. Be ready for the road, though. The TF-436 is about 22 kilometres of hairpins, barely two cars wide in places, and one of the more demanding scenic drives in Europe. Parking in Masca is very limited.

The other way to see this coastline is from the water. Boat trips out of Los Gigantes run beneath cliffs up to around 500 metres high, take two to three hours, start from roughly €30, and many anchor for a swim in Masca bay, the same cove the hikers walk down to. Kayak and snorkel trips run about €35 to €65. Same scenery, no four-hour descent, and you can bring the children. More on both in our guide to Los Gigantes and Masca and on our west Tenerife page.

Good to know

Masca gorge hike FAQ

How much does the Masca gorge hike cost?

The trail permit starts at about €40.66 per adult and roughly €20.33 for ages 8 to 17, and it is free for Canary Islands residents. The boat out of Masca bay is extra and bought separately. Prices have changed before, so check the official site at caminobarrancodemasca.com before booking.

Can I drive to the start of the Masca trail?

No. Hikers must arrive on the TITSA route 355 bus from Santiago del Teide, and arriving at the trailhead by any other vehicle is prohibited. Park in Santiago del Teide and take the bus, allowing about an hour before your booked slot.

Do I have to book a boat as well as the permit?

Yes. The gorge is one-way and you leave by boat from Masca bay to Los Gigantes. The boat ticket is a separate booking and you must have it before you are allowed onto the trail. Without it, access is refused.

Can children do the Masca gorge hike?

Children under 8 are not admitted at all, and older children must be with an adult. Beyond the rule, it is a four-hour descent on rough ground in the heat, so think carefully about whether a child would actually enjoy it rather than endure it.

What should I wear and bring?

Closed mountain hiking boots are mandatory and are checked at the entrance, so trainers and sandals will get you turned away. Bring plenty of water, sun cover, food, and photo ID that matches your booking. A helmet is loaned to you as part of the permit.
