Choosing your trip

Tenerife or Gran Canaria — which should you choose?

Home› Questions› Tenerife or Gran Canaria

Short answer

Both deliver reliable winter sun and cost about the same. Choose Tenerife if you want dramatic landscape, Spain's highest mountain and more variety in a single trip. Choose Gran Canaria if you want the famous Maspalomas dunes, a genuinely walkable city beach at Las Palmas, and everything packed into a smaller area.

Landscape and variety

Tenerife is the larger island and by some distance the more dramatic. Teide rises to 3,715 metres, the Anaga range in the north-east holds an ancient laurel forest, and the Los Gigantes cliffs fall five hundred metres straight into the sea. You can be on a beach in the morning and above the clouds by lunchtime, and the north and south feel like different countries.

Gran Canaria is often described as a continent in miniature, and it earns it — but on a smaller canvas. Its signature landscape is the Maspalomas dunes in the south, a genuine desert of sand beside the sea, which Tenerife has nothing to match.

Resorts, beaches and nightlife

Gran Canaria concentrates its tourism in the south around Maspalomas and Playa del Inglés, which between them hold huge beaches and the best-established LGBT+ scene in the archipelago. Las Palmas in the north is the real difference-maker: a full-sized city with Playa de las Canteras, a long urban beach good enough to swim at daily, which no Tenerife city offers.

Tenerife spreads its resorts along the south-west coast, from the upmarket Costa Adeje through the busy Playa de las Américas to quieter Los Cristianos, with the older, more Canarian Puerto de la Cruz in the north. Its beaches are a mix of imported golden sand in the south and natural black volcanic sand elsewhere.

Which one for which trip

For a first visit to the Canaries with a hire car and an appetite for scenery, Tenerife gives you more to see and the single most impressive thing in the archipelago in Teide. For a holiday centred on one very good beach with less driving, Gran Canaria is the easier proposition.

Weather is close enough not to decide it. Both have the same pattern: sunnier and drier in the south, cooler and greener in the north. Both are reachable on the same flights and cost broadly the same. There is also a ferry between them, so a trip taking in both is entirely practical if you have two weeks.

  • Tenerife: Teide, Anaga, Los Gigantes, more variety
  • Gran Canaria: Maspalomas dunes, Las Palmas city beach
  • Weather and cost are close to identical
  • Ferries link the two if you want both
Also asked

Related questions

Is Tenerife or Gran Canaria better for families?

Both work well. Tenerife has Siam Park and Loro Parque, two of the strongest attractions in the islands. Gran Canaria has larger, calmer southern beaches concentrated in a smaller area, which means less driving.

Which island has better weather?

They are very similar. On both, the south is sunnier and drier and the north is cooler and cloudier. Neither has a meaningful advantage.

Can you visit both on one trip?

Yes. Ferries run between the islands, and it is a realistic plan if you have two weeks. For one week, pick one.
