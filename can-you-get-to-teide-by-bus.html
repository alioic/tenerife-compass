Can you get to Mount Teide by bus?

Home› Questions› Teide by bus

Short answer

Yes. TITSA line 342 runs up from Costa Adeje and line 348 from Puerto de la Cruz, each once a day — up in the morning, back in the afternoon. It works, and it is cheap, but it fixes your day to a single window of roughly four hours in the park.

The two services, and what they actually give you

Line 342 climbs from Costa Adeje, picking up along the southern resorts, and line 348 comes up from Puerto de la Cruz on the north side. Both run once each way per day: up in the morning, back in the early to mid afternoon. Between them they cover the main stops on the crater floor, including the cable car base station and the Roques de García.

That single daily return is the whole catch. You get roughly four hours in the park, and you cannot extend it — miss the bus down and there is no later one. It is enough for the cable car and a short walk, or for the Roques de García loop and lunch, but not comfortably for both.

What the bus does not include

The bus takes you to the park, not up the mountain. The cable car is a separate ticket and should be booked online in advance, particularly in peak season, because walk-up availability is unreliable and you cannot afford to queue when your ride home leaves at a fixed time.

Going above the cable car's top station to the actual crater rim needs a free permit, booked separately through the national parks reservation site, and the daily quota goes early. If that is your plan, the bus is a tight way to do it — a hire car or a tour gives you the slack you will want.

Check the current timetable before you commit. TITSA adjusts these services seasonally, and an out-of-date departure time is the single most common way this trip goes wrong.

  • One return service per day on each line
  • Roughly four hours in the park
  • Cable car ticket booked separately
  • Summit permit is free but must be pre-booked

When a tour or a car makes more sense

If you want the sunset above the clouds, or the stargazing, the bus cannot help you — it is long gone. Organised tours from the southern resorts cover exactly that gap, and the sunset-and-stars trips are the reason most people book one.

A hire car gives you the most freedom and costs the least per head if there are three or four of you, at the price of driving mountain roads. The TF-21 from Vilaflor and the TF-38 are well surfaced but continuously winding, and it is a long climb. Whatever you arrive in, dress for below freezing: the crater floor sits above two thousand metres and behaves nothing like the coast.

Also asked

Related questions

How much does the bus to Teide cost?

A few euros each way on TITSA, which makes it by far the cheapest way up. Check current fares, and consider a ten-trip card if you will use the buses elsewhere.

How long do you get at Teide with the bus?

Roughly four hours, fixed by the single daily return. Enough for the cable car or the Roques de García, but tight for both.

Can you do Teide sunset or stargazing by bus?

No. The buses return in the afternoon. Evening trips need a hire car or an organised tour.

Do the buses go to the top of Teide?

No. They stop on the crater floor, including at the cable car base station. The cable car takes you higher, and the final path to the summit needs a separate free permit.
