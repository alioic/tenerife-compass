Do you tip in Tenerife?

Home› Questions› Tipping

Short answer

Not obligatory, and nobody will chase you. Spanish menu prices include tax and service, so a tip is a genuine extra rather than a top-up of someone's wage. Rounding up the bill, or leaving five to ten per cent after a good meal, is generous by local standards.

What locals actually do

Considerably less than visitors assume. In an everyday bar or café, Canarians typically leave the small change from the bill and nothing more. After a proper restaurant meal they might round up to the nearest note, or leave a few euros on a larger table if the service was good.

The reason is structural rather than mean-spirited. Spanish hospitality staff are paid a wage rather than a tipped minimum, and the price on the menu is the price you pay, tax and service included. Nobody's income depends on your arithmetic, which takes the anxiety out of the whole thing.

It is worth unlearning American habits here in particular. A twenty per cent tip is not generous in Tenerife so much as conspicuous, and staff in the resort areas have simply learned that some visitors do it. Nobody is offended, but nobody is expecting it either.

A rough guide by situation

Restaurants: round up, or five to ten per cent for service you actually noticed. Ten is at the generous end. Bars and cafés: leave the coins. Taxis: round up to the next euro or two — meters are used and the fare is the fare.

Hotels: entirely optional. A euro or two for a porter, or a few left for housekeeping at the end of a stay, is a kind gesture rather than an expectation. Boat crews and walking guides: a few euros each if you enjoyed the trip, and they will not be surprised either way.

Two things worth watching

Check the bill before adding anything. A minority of restaurants in the busiest tourist streets add a service charge, and if servicio is already on there, adding ten per cent on top is simply paying twice. It is not a scam, just something to read.

The other is newer: card machines in tourist areas that present a screen of pre-set tip percentages before you pay. That prompt is an imported habit, not a Canarian one, and there is no awkwardness in choosing the no-tip option or handing over cash instead. Tipping here is a thank-you, not a tax.

  • Service and tax are already in the price
  • Read the bill for servicio before adding more
  • Card-machine tip prompts are an import, not a custom
  • Cash is easier for the person actually serving you
Also asked

Related questions

Is it rude not to tip in Tenerife?

No. Leaving nothing in a bar is completely normal, and in a restaurant nobody will comment. Tipping is appreciated rather than expected.

How much do you tip a taxi driver in Tenerife?

Rounding up to the next euro or two is plenty. Taxis are metered and there is no expectation beyond that.

Do you tip in all-inclusive hotels in Tenerife?

Not required. Some guests leave a few euros for bar or restaurant staff they see repeatedly, or for housekeeping at the end of the stay. It is a gesture, not an obligation.

Should I tip in cash or on the card?

Cash, if you can. It reaches the person who served you more reliably than a figure added to a card payment.
