Do you need a car in Tenerife?

If your holiday is a hotel, a beach and a few good dinners, you can skip the car entirely; if you want to see the island behind the resorts, hire one for part of the week.

Home› Do you need a car

The short answer

No for a resort holiday in the south; yes if you want to explore the island properly.

Most people staying in Costa Adeje for a week hire a car, use it twice, and pay for five days of parking they did not need. Others spend a fortnight here and never see the caldera or the laurel forest because the bus times did not work. Both are avoidable, and the fix is the same: decide what kind of trip you are having before you book anything.

The case against

When you do not need one

The southern resorts were built to be walked. A flat, paved seafront promenade runs from Los Cristianos through Playa de las Américas and Costa Adeje to Playa del Duque and on to La Caleta, close to 10 km end to end, passing beach after beach with bars, benches and shops. It is stroller and wheelchair friendly the whole way. If your hotel sits on that strip, everything you want in a normal day is a walk away, and the walk is one of the nicer things about the area.

When you do not want to walk, taxis are white, metered and everywhere. Resort hops cost a few euros; the airport run is €25 to €45. Take four of those in a week and you have still spent less than a hire car plus fuel plus hotel parking. The buses are genuinely good too, which surprises people. For a first visit built around sun, sea and a couple of organised day trips, a car is a cost and a parking problem rather than a convenience. The case for

When a car earns its keep

Tenerife's best days out are in places the coaches either skip or arrive at by eleven with two hundred people. A car buys you early mornings and empty roads, which here is the whole game.

The places that genuinely reward driving yourself: Teide National Park, where the crater floor at sunrise is a different experience from the midday cable car queue; Anaga, whose villages and laurel forest reward wandering rather than an itinerary; Masca and Teno; the quieter western villages like Alcalá and Playa San Juan; Garachico and the northern towns; and remote beaches such as La Tejita. None are impossible without a car. All are better with one.

The middle path most people never consider: hire for two or three days rather than seven. Pick your weather, drive the days you want to drive, walk the rest.

Decide quickly

Which one are you?

Skip the car if...

  • You are staying on the seafront strip between Los Cristianos and La Caleta
  • You booked all-inclusive and plan to use it
  • You are here for a short break of three or four nights
  • You would rather not drive on holiday, or nobody in the group is comfortable on mountain roads
  • Your hotel charges extra for parking and you would be paying twice
  • One or two organised excursions cover everything on your list

Hire a car if...

  • Teide, Anaga, Masca or the north are on your list
  • You want to be somewhere at sunrise or stay for sunset
  • You are staying inland, in the west, or anywhere off the main resort strip
  • You are travelling with small children and hate coach timetables
  • You are here two weeks or more
  • You care about eating where the tour buses do not stop

What it costs

Rates swing enormously with season, and the headline price is rarely the final one.

Yearly average€20–25A fair number to budget with if your dates are flexible.
Peak (December, Easter, August)€35+ (quoted in sterling by UK aggregators)Book early. Automatics and larger cars sell out first.

Two things matter more than the daily rate. Check the fuel policy and insist on full-to-full, because pre-paid tank deals almost always cost more. And decide on excess-waiver insurance before you reach the desk, where it is sold at a markup. The local firm Cicar is well regarded. Comparing across aggregators is the fastest way to see what your dates actually cost.

Honestly

What the driving is actually like

Easier than people expect, most of the time. The TF-1 is a free, modern, toll-free motorway running the length of the south and east coast, and there are no tolls anywhere on the island. Driving is on the right, roads are well maintained, and the mountain roads up to Teide are winding but properly surfaced.

The friction is parking, not driving. Resort street parking is tight in peak season, blue-line zones are paid, and many hotels charge separately for a space. Factor that in before assuming a week's hire is good value.

Then the hard case, said plainly. The TF-436 to Masca is one of Europe's most demanding scenic drives: about 22 km, barely two cars wide in places, relentless hairpins and steep drops, genuinely stressful when a coach comes the other way. Confident drivers only, and go early. Anaga's TF-12 and TF-134 have the same character, spectacular but narrow and endlessly winding, about an hour from Santa Cruz to Benijo, with very limited parking at weekends. If that description makes you tense, it is telling you something useful.

One place you cannot drive at all is Punta de Teno: the access road is closed to private vehicles every day. Take bus 369 from Buenavista del Norte, about €1, or an authorised taxi.

Without a car

The bus alternative

TITSA's green buses, the guaguas, are clean, cheap and reliable, and reach far more of the island than visitors assume. Pay the driver cash, or tap a Ten+ (tenmás) contactless card: about €2 as a one-off, topped up as you go, shareable between passengers, and it saves roughly 20 to 30% on every fare. Buy one on day one. Local hops run about €1.45 to €2, intercity legs about €3 to €4 at card rates. Lines 111 and 343 serve the airport and Santa Cruz, the 110/111 express from Costa Adeje to Santa Cruz is about €9.45 single, the 470 runs the east coast, and the 342 goes to Teide.

That last one carries the caveat that decides the whole question for many people. Line 342 runs from Estación Costa Adeje to the Teide cable car and El Portillo, roughly two hours each way, but there is exactly one departure up and one back per day. Your day is built around that timetable, and you should buy the return when you board. Thousands of people do it happily. It is also the strongest single argument for hiring a car for one day. Good to know

Car hire FAQ

Do you need a car in Tenerife?

Not for a resort holiday in the south. The seafront promenade from Los Cristianos to La Caleta is close to 10 km of flat paved walking, taxis are cheap and metered, and the TITSA buses are reliable. You need a car if you want Teide, Anaga, Masca, the west or the northern towns on your own schedule rather than a coach's.

How much does car hire cost in Tenerife?

Roughly €10 to €15 a day in low season such as June, around €20 to €25 a day as a yearly average, and around €35 or more a day at peak times: December, Easter and August. Always check the fuel policy, choose full-to-full, and sort excess-waiver insurance before you arrive at the desk.

Can you get to Mount Teide without a car?

Yes, on TITSA line 342 from Estación Costa Adeje to the cable car and El Portillo, about two hours each way. The catch is that there is only one departure up and one back each day, so the whole day is built around that timetable. Buy the return when you board. Otherwise, take an organised tour or hire a car for the day.

Is driving in Tenerife difficult?

Mostly no. The TF-1 motorway is modern and toll-free, there are no tolls anywhere on the island, driving is on the right and roads are in good condition. The exceptions are the TF-436 to Masca, about 22 km of narrow relentless hairpins that is one of Europe's most demanding scenic drives, and the TF-12 and TF-134 through Anaga. Go early and take them slowly.

What is a Ten+ card and is it worth it?

Ten+ (tenmás) is TITSA's contactless travel card. It costs about €2 as a one-off, you top it up as you go, several passengers can share one, and it cuts fares by roughly 20 to 30%. If you plan more than a couple of bus journeys, it pays for itself almost immediately.
