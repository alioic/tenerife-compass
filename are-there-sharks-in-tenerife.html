Are there sharks in Tenerife?

Home› Questions› Sharks

Short answer

Yes, but not the kind you are worried about. Canary waters hold several shark species, most notably the harmless seabed-dwelling angelshark. Attacks are essentially unheard of. The genuine sea risks here are currents, swell and the occasional Portuguese man o' war.

What is actually out there

The Canary Islands sit in the open Atlantic, so sharks pass through — that is true of every ocean coastline on Earth. The species most associated with these islands is the angelshark, Squatina squatina, which looks more like a ray than a shark and spends its life half-buried on sandy bottoms. It is critically endangered across most of its former range, and Canary waters are one of its last strongholds, which makes divers here rather pleased to see one.

Large predatory sharks exist in the wider Atlantic but stay in deep water well away from the resort beaches. Attacks on swimmers in the Canaries are so rare that they are not a factor any visitor needs to plan around.

What you should actually be careful of

The Atlantic itself is the risk, and it is a real one. Beaches on the north and west coasts face open ocean with strong currents and heavy swell, and several — Benijo and the Anaga beaches in particular — have no lifeguard cover. The flag system is not decorative: red means do not enter, and people drown here every year ignoring it.

The second genuine hazard is the Portuguese man o' war, known locally as the carabela portuguesa. These drift in on certain wind conditions, sometimes in numbers, and their sting is severe. When they arrive, beaches fly the appropriate warning and close. Do not touch them on the sand either, since detached tentacles still sting.

  • Follow the beach flags without exception
  • North and west beaches have strong currents
  • Portuguese man o' war stings are serious
  • Sheltered southern beaches are the safest swimming

If you actually want to see marine life

The waters off the south-west coast hold resident pilot whales and bottlenose dolphins year-round, which is unusual and is why whale-watching trips from Los Cristianos, Puerto Colón and Los Gigantes have such a high success rate. Trips run from around thirty euros.

For anything smaller, snorkelling and diving are best on the sheltered south and west coasts. Divers hoping for an angelshark encounter usually go in winter, when the animals come into shallower sandy bays.

Related questions

Has there ever been a shark attack in Tenerife?

Attacks are essentially unheard of in the Canary Islands. Sharks are present in Atlantic waters, but incidents involving swimmers are not something visitors need to plan around.

What is an angelshark?

A flat, bottom-dwelling shark that resembles a ray. It is critically endangered globally, and Canary waters are one of its last strongholds. It is harmless to swimmers.

Are there jellyfish in Tenerife?

Sometimes, and the Portuguese man o' war — not a true jellyfish — is the one that matters. Their arrival depends on wind and currents, and beaches fly warnings and close when they appear.

Is it safe to swim in the sea in Tenerife?

On the sheltered southern beaches, generally yes. On the exposed north and west coasts, conditions can be dangerous and lifeguard cover is patchy. Always follow the flags.
