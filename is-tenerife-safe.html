Is Tenerife safe?

Home› Questions› Safety

Short answer

Yes. Tenerife is part of Spain and is a very safe place to travel, with violent crime rare and resorts heavily policed. The genuine risks are not criminal: the Atlantic, the mountain roads and the sun account for far more visitor trouble than anything else.

The sea is the real risk

If one thing on this page matters, it is this. Tenerife's beaches range from sheltered southern bays where children paddle safely to open Atlantic coastline with heavy swell, powerful currents and no lifeguard on duty. People get into difficulty here every year, and it is almost always at the second kind of beach.

Treat the flag system as an instruction rather than a suggestion. Green means normal conditions, yellow means care, red means do not enter — and red means it whether or not other people are in the water. On the north and west coasts, and at the Anaga beaches in particular, assume there is no lifeguard unless you can see one. If you want reliable swimming, the sheltered southern beaches are genuinely safe.

Driving, and the roads that catch people out

The motorways are modern and easy. The mountain roads are a different proposition: the TF-436 to Masca, the Anaga roads and the climbs to Teide are narrow, relentlessly winding and often have a drop on one side and a coach coming the other way. They are not dangerous if you are unhurried and sober, but they punish over-confidence.

Two practical points. Hire-car break-ins at remote trailhead car parks happen often enough to be worth planning around — leave nothing visible, ever. And driving those roads tired after a late night is where the actual risk sits, not in the driving itself.

Crime, and the one thing that annoys people

Petty theft is the realistic concern: pickpocketing in crowded resort areas, bags lifted from beaches, things taken from cars. Standard precautions cover it. Violent crime against visitors is rare, and walking back to your hotel at night in the southern resorts is not something to worry about.

The thing that genuinely irritates people is not crime at all: timeshare and holiday-club touts working the busy stretches of Playa de las Américas and Los Cristianos, usually opening with a scratch card or a survey. A firm no and continuing to walk ends it. Do not accept a lift to a presentation, however good the prize sounds.

  • Emergency number is 112, as everywhere in the EU
  • Follow the beach flags without exception
  • Leave nothing visible in a parked car
  • Walk past the scratch-card touts
Also asked

Related questions

Is Tenerife safe at night?

Yes, in normal terms. The resort areas are busy and policed. Ordinary city sense applies, and the busiest nightlife strip in Playa de las Américas gets rowdy late, which is worth knowing when choosing a hotel.

Is it safe to swim in Tenerife?

On the sheltered southern beaches, yes. On the open north and west coasts, conditions can be genuinely dangerous and lifeguard cover is patchy. Always follow the flags.

What is the emergency number in Tenerife?

112, the same across the EU. It covers police, ambulance and fire and has English-speaking operators.

Are the timeshare touts a scam?

The scratch-card approach is a sales tactic, not a robbery. It costs you a long high-pressure presentation rather than your wallet. Decline and keep walking.
