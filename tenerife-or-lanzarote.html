Choosing your trip

Tenerife or Lanzarote — which should you choose?

Home› Questions› Tenerife or Lanzarote

Short answer

Choose Tenerife if you want variety — Teide, laurel forest, cities, nightlife and enough to fill two weeks. Choose Lanzarote if you want a stark volcanic landscape, César Manrique's architecture and a quieter, lower-rise island where nothing is more than an hour away.

Two very different landscapes

Lanzarote looks like nowhere else in Europe. The eruptions of the 1730s buried a third of the island and left the Timanfaya badlands, a field of cinder cones and frozen lava that is still hot enough underground to be demonstrated to visitors. Vines grow in pits dug into black ash. It is austere, and people either love it immediately or find it bleak.

Tenerife is larger and far more varied. Teide rises to 3,715 metres, the Anaga range holds a subtropical laurel forest, and the north is green in a way Lanzarote never is. You can have a desert morning and a forest afternoon, which is not an option on the smaller island.

What there is to do

Tenerife wins on volume. Siam Park and Loro Parque are the best attractions in the archipelago, whale watching runs year-round off the south-west coast, and Santa Cruz and La Laguna give you proper cities with markets, tapas and a carnival second only to Rio. If rain or wind spoils a day, there is an alternative.

Lanzarote's draw is more curated. The island's identity was shaped by one man: the artist César Manrique, whose interventions — the Jameos del Agua caves, the Mirador del Río, the Cactus Garden — are genuinely worth the trip, and whose influence produced the building rules that keep the island low-rise and largely free of high-rise blocks. Playa Papagayo in the south is as good a beach as either island offers.

Weather, wind and getting around

Both are warm year-round. Lanzarote is drier and noticeably windier, which suits watersports and suits sunbathing rather less — the beaches often have windbreaks for a reason. Tenerife's south is more sheltered; its north is cooler and cloudier than anywhere on Lanzarote.

Lanzarote is small enough to see properly in a week with a car, and nowhere is much more than an hour away. Tenerife is big enough that a week means choosing, and two weeks is not too many. If you want to lie still near one very good beach, Lanzarote makes it easy. If you want to keep moving, Tenerife gives you more to move towards.

  • Lanzarote: Timanfaya, Manrique, low-rise, windier
  • Tenerife: Teide, forests, cities, nightlife, more variety
  • Both warm all year; Lanzarote drier and breezier
  • Lanzarote fits a week; Tenerife rewards two
Also asked

Related questions

Which is better for a first Canary Islands trip?

Tenerife, for most people — it has more variety and more to fall back on. Lanzarote is the better choice if the volcanic landscape and Manrique's work are what draw you.

Is Lanzarote windier than Tenerife?

Generally yes. Lanzarote is flatter and more exposed, and beach windbreaks are common. Tenerife's south-west coast is more sheltered.

Which has better beaches?

Lanzarote's Papagayo beaches are outstanding and largely undeveloped. Tenerife has more beaches overall and more serviced ones, with imported golden sand in the south and black volcanic sand elsewhere.
