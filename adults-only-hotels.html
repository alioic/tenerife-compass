The best adults-only hotels in Tenerife

No inflatables in the pool, no 7am wake-up from the room next door. But adults-only covers everything from silent spa hotels to rooftop pool parties, so it pays to know which one you are booking.

Start here

Why Tenerife does this well

The island has a deep bench of adults-only hotels, most of them along the south-west coast where the weather is most reliable. That competition keeps prices sane: €300 a night for a design flagship in La Caleta, or €130 for a four-star spa hotel with its feet in the sand at Los Cristianos.

The ten below are spread across the island, so there is something here whether you want the polished south-west, the windy east or the greener north. Prices are approximate starting rates outside peak weeks.

How we picked these

Every hotel here is genuinely adults-only, and every star rating has been checked rather than copied from a booking page. We favoured a real location advantage, on the beach or in the old town, over inland places with a shuttle bus, and we say plainly where a hotel is lively rather than calm. We have not stayed in all ten; where a detail could not be confirmed, we left it out.

What "adults-only" actually means here

Two things vary between hotels, and both catch people out.

The age limit is not always 18. Tigotán Lovers & Friends and KN Arenas del Mar are strict 18+. Royal Hideaway Corales Beach, Iberostar Grand El Mirador and AluaSoul Costa Adeje are 16+. That cuts both ways: some will take a 17-year-old, and if you booked specifically to avoid teenagers, the 16+ hotels will not guarantee that. Check the age rule on the hotel's own page before you pay, because chains change these policies quietly.

The atmosphere varies more than the label suggests. Adults-only is a marketing category, not a promise of silence. Iberostar Grand El Mirador, Spring Arona Gran and Hotel San Roque are the calm, spa-led end: quiet pools, long dinners, early nights. Tigotán is the opposite, a social hotel with a rooftop infinity pool and a crowd that came for exactly that. Riu Arecas and AluaSoul sit in between. If you want a hushed pool deck, do not book the hotel with the DJ.

One practical note: these hotels fill earlier than family resorts for the winter sun season, December to February. The five-stars go first.

Royal Hideaway Corales Beach

Barceló's adults-only flagship on the La Caleta seafront, and the most architecturally ambitious hotel on the coast: the building curls like a wave. It rates among the highest on the island, and the wider Corales complex includes Michelin-starred dining. Ages 16 and over.

Iberostar Grand El Mirador

All-suite, seafront and refined in a slightly old-school way: the quiet choice at the top of the market, with a proper spa and a crowd here to do very little. It also trades as JOIA El Mirador. Ages 16 and over.

San Roque
Adults only

Hotel San Roque

An 18th-century merchant's house in Garachico turned into a design hotel, with Mackintosh furniture, art and a courtyard pool. The most distinctive place on this list. The trade-off is real: the north is cooler and cloudier and the sea rougher, so book it for the town, not for sunbathing.

Riu Arecas
Adults only

Hotel Riu Arecas

Beachfront in Costa Adeje, recently refitted, and a popular all-inclusive that does the fundamentals well. Not a design statement, but you are on the smart end of the coast with the sea across the road for a fraction of five-star money. Busy rather than serene.

Arona Gran
Adults only

Spring Arona Gran Hotel & Spa

Sits directly on Los Cristianos beach with bay views, big pools, a sauna and a gym. The best-value adults-only hotel on the coast, and genuinely walkable: old town, harbour and dozens of restaurants all on foot. Calm and grown-up, no gimmicks.

Barceló Santiago

Perched on the clifftop at Puerto de Santiago, with the Los Gigantes cliffs on one side and La Gomera on the horizon. It is the best-known hotel in the west, and the west gets the best sunsets on the island; you watch them from the pool terrace. Quieter area, so plan on a car.

AluaSoul Costa Adeje

Renovated in 2024 and now part of the Hyatt Inclusive Collection, so the rooms feel current rather than dated, about 200 m from the Torviscas and La Pinta beaches. Worth flagging that it is 16+ rather than strict 18+, which gives it a younger, less hushed feel than its price rivals.

Sunprime Coral Suites & Spa

Tigotán
Adults only

Tigotán Lovers & Friends

The social one. Design-forward, strict 18+, and built around a rooftop infinity pool where the point is very much to be seen. Book it if you want a holiday with a soundtrack and other people to talk to; avoid it if your idea of adults-only is a book and a lounger. Good value for what it is.

Arenas del Mar

KN Hotel Arenas del Mar

A modern 18+ spa hotel right on the water by La Jaquita beach in El Médano, with front-row views of Montaña Roja. The cheapest on this list, with one honest caveat: the east coast is windy. That is why the kitesurfers are there, and why sunbathing is less pleasant than in Costa Adeje.

Location

Which area suits couples

The hotel matters, but the neighbourhood you walk out into at 8pm matters more.

Costa Adeje and La Caleta are the upscale, calm end of the south and the default answer for most couples. La Caleta is a former fishing hamlet with the best seafood on the coast and essentially no nightlife, which is the point. Costa Adeje is polished and easy to walk, but it covers a lot of ground, so check how far your hotel really is from the sand. Full breakdown: where to stay in South Tenerife.

Playa de las Américas is livelier and more central, with the widest range of prices and the island's nightlife. It splits in two: the Verónicas end is loud, the southern end around Playa del Camisón much calmer. If you are torn between the two big resorts, we compare them in Costa Adeje vs Playa de las Américas. Los Cristianos is the value option, an old fishing town with a working harbour and a sheltered beach that still feels like somewhere people live.

The west, around Puerto de Santiago and Los Gigantes, has the best sunsets and the most dramatic coastline, but it is quieter and you will want a car: see the west Tenerife guide. The north is the contrarian choice, greener and more Canarian with better food and worse beach weather. If old towns and laurel forest appeal more than a lounger, read the north Tenerife guide before booking the south by reflex.

Good to know

Adults-only hotels FAQ

Are all adults-only hotels in Tenerife 18+?

No, and this is the most common misunderstanding. Tigotán Lovers & Friends and KN Hotel Arenas del Mar are strict 18+. Royal Hideaway Corales Beach, Iberostar Grand El Mirador and AluaSoul Costa Adeje set the bar at 16, and some smaller hotels go lower still. Check the minimum age on the hotel's own website before booking.

Which is the best adults-only hotel in Tenerife?

Royal Hideaway Corales Beach in La Caleta, from around €300 a night: a five-star, design-led seafront hotel that rates among the highest on the island, with Michelin-starred dining in the wider complex. For the same money but a calmer feel, book Iberostar Grand El Mirador in Costa Adeje.

What is the best-value adults-only hotel in Tenerife?

Spring Arona Gran Hotel & Spa in Los Cristianos, from roughly €130 a night. It is a four-star directly on the beach with bay views, large pools, a sauna and a gym, and you can walk to the old town and harbour. KN Hotel Arenas del Mar in El Médano is cheaper again, from around €94, but the east coast is noticeably windy.

Are adults-only hotels in Tenerife actually quiet?

Some are, some are not. Iberostar Grand El Mirador, Spring Arona Gran and Hotel San Roque are the calm, spa-focused end. Tigotán Lovers & Friends is deliberately social, with a rooftop infinity pool and music. Riu Arecas and AluaSoul sit in between. The label is not a promise of silence.

Where should couples stay in Tenerife?

Costa Adeje and La Caleta for calm, good restaurants and the highest concentration of upmarket adults-only hotels. Playa de las Américas if you want nightlife and a central base. Los Cristianos for value and a sheltered beach. Puerto de Santiago in the west for cliff views and the island's best sunsets. Garachico in the north if character and food matter more to you than beach weather.
