South Tenerife

The best things to do in South Tenerife

The sunny south is where most visitors base themselves, and it is the launchpad for nearly every big Tenerife experience: whales offshore, a volcano an hour inland, the best water park in the world and a coastline of Blue Flag beaches. Here is what is genuinely worth your time and money, with real prices and honest opinions on the rest.

The big-ticket experiences

If you only book three things in the south, make it these. Two are near-guaranteed crowd-pleasers and one is a bucket-list volcano.

Whale & dolphin watching
#1 rated

Whale & dolphin watching

Resident pilot whales and dolphins live in the strait off the south coast all year, so sightings are close to guaranteed rather than a gamble. This is the single best-value experience in Tenerife. Skip the cheapest party boats and pick a licensed "Barco Azul" (Blue Boat) operator that keeps a respectful distance.

From ~€22 · 2–3 hrs · Puerto Colón / Los Cristianos

Check availability
Siam Park
Bestseller

Siam Park

Regularly voted the world's best water park, and it earns it. The Tower of Power drops you almost vertically through a shark tank, the Dragon is genuinely terrifying and the wave pool is huge. Worth it: yes, easily a full day. Go at opening to beat queues, and consider the fast-pass in July and August.

From ~€40 · full day · Costa Adeje

Check availability
Mount Teide & stargazing
Bucket list

Mount Teide & stargazing

Spain's highest peak at 3,715m, a UNESCO site with Starlight-certified skies. The cable car gets you near the top in eight minutes, but the standout is a sunset-and-stargazing tour: dinner, telescopes and some of the clearest night skies in Europe. Dress warm, it is cold and thin-aired up there year-round.

From ~€43 · cable car; stargazing ~€60–110 · centre of the island

Check availability

Book ahead

Mount Teide's summit permit is free but must be reserved weeks in advance, and the Barranco del Infierno hike and Masca gorge also need pre-booking. The cable car can close in high wind.

Boat trips & water

The south coast and the dramatic Los Gigantes cliffs to the northwest are the base for almost everything on the water, from lazy catamaran lunches to jet skis and a real submarine.

Masca & Los Gigantes
Year-round

Masca gorge & Los Gigantes cliffs

The most spectacular scenery on the island: sheer cliffs rising up to 600m straight from the sea. Options range from a guided descent of the Masca gorge with a boat pick-up, to gentler kayak-and-snorkel trips under the cliffs. The gorge hike is steep, about 3.5 hours one way, and you exit by boat.

From ~€45 · half day · Los Gigantes (NW, ~45–60 min drive)

Check availability
Catamaran cruise
Bestseller

Catamaran sunset cruise

A relaxed half-day with a swim stop, snacks and often lunch and drinks included. Good value and a soft way to see dolphins without a dedicated whale-watching trip. The sunset sailings are the most atmospheric and book out fastest in summer.

From ~€25 · 3 hrs · Puerto Colón

Check availability
Submarine Safari
Unusual

Submarine Safari

A genuine submarine that dives to around 30m off Las Galletas, not a glass-bottom gimmick. Pricey for the length, but it is one of very few real subs open to tourists in Europe and a hit with kids and non-divers.

From ~€55 · ~2 hrs · Las Galletas

Jet ski safari
Adrenaline

Jet ski safari

Guided jet ski runs along the coast out of Puerto Colón, from short blasts to longer safaris. Fun if you want speed on the water. Prices climb quickly with duration, so check exactly how long you are actually riding.

From ~€50 · 20–40 min · Puerto Colón

Parasailing
Adrenaline

Parasailing

Up over the bay on a parachute behind a boat, with big coastal views and a soft landing back on deck. Short but memorable, and easy to combine with a jet ski session from the same marina.

From ~€55 · ~15 min flight · Puerto Colón

Scuba diving
For beginners

Discover Scuba diving

The warm, clear Atlantic and calm south-coast dive sites make this a great place to try scuba for the first time. A Discover Scuba session includes training and a shallow dive with an instructor, no certification needed. Worth it if you have ever been curious.

Family & animal parks

Beyond Siam Park there is a cluster of animal and water parks. A quick honest note: Loro Parque is the famous one, but it is a full day up in the north.

Loro Parque
#1 rated

Loro Parque (north day-trip)

Consistently rated one of the best zoos in the world, with orcas, dolphins, penguins, gorillas and parrots. The catch: it is in Puerto de la Cruz, a 1h–1h15 drive north, so it is a committed full day. Book the version that includes coach transport and skip the parking and navigation.

From ~€44 · full day · Puerto de la Cruz (north)

Check availability
Aqualand
Year-round

Aqualand Costa Adeje

The south's second water park and a solid choice if Siam Park is too intense for younger kids, or if you just want a cheaper splash day. It also has a dolphin show. Fewer white-knuckle slides than Siam, but calmer and easier with small children.

From ~€30 · full day · Costa Adeje

Check availability
Jungle Park
Family

Jungle Park / Las Águilas

A jungle-themed animal park with bird-of-prey and sea-lion shows and a toboggan run down the hillside. Good half to full day for families, and the raptor flying display is the highlight worth timing your visit around.

From ~€26 · €18 child · half–full day · Arona

Check availability
Monkey Park
Family

Monkey Park

A small, hands-on wildlife park near Los Cristianos where you can feed marmosets and lemurs up close. It is modest and a bit old-school, but young children love it and it is cheap and quick. An easy add-on rather than a full day.

From ~€10 · 1–2 hrs · near Los Cristianos

Check availability
Camel Park
Family

Camel Park Arona

Camel rides through volcanic scenery plus a small petting-farm area. Novelty more than a must-do, but it fits neatly into a half-day when combined with a nearby stop, and the setting inland is genuinely scenic.

From ~€12 · 1–2 hrs · Arona

Adrenaline

Buggy & quad safaris

Off-road convoys into the hills and volcanic tracks, usually around three hours. The sunset trips that finish with a glass of cava are the pick. You will get dusty, so wear old clothes and a buff for your face.

Free & cheap: beaches, walks & towns

You do not need to book anything to have a great day in the south. The beaches are all free (a sunbed rents for about €10–15) and the coastal walk is one of the best on the island.

The best beaches

  • Playa del Duque — the upscale Blue Flag beach in Costa Adeje, golden sand and smart beach clubs.
  • Playa Fañabé — a 2km Blue Flag family beach with easy facilities and gentle water.
  • Playa de las Vistas (Los Cristianos) — wide, calm and Blue Flag, imported pale sand, the most swimmer-friendly beach in the south.
  • Playa El Bobo / La Pinta — smaller coves with some of the better swimming near the marina.
  • La Tejita (near El Médano) — a long, natural, undeveloped beach; windy and part-naturist, with Montaña Roja behind it.
  • Los Cristianos beach — sheltered and central, handy for a quick swim between other plans.

Walks & towns

  • The coastal promenade — you can walk almost 10km along the seafront from Los Cristianos through Las Américas and Costa Adeje out to La Caleta. Do it early morning or at sunset.
  • La Caleta — a small fishing village at the quiet end of the promenade, best known for its seafood restaurants right on the water.
  • Adeje old town — a real Canarian town above the resorts, with the Iglesia de Santa Úrsula, the historic Casa Fuerte and the trailhead for the Barranco del Infierno.
  • Torviscas / Fañabé market — held Thursday and Saturday, roughly 9am–2pm on Avenida de Bruselas. Note it is not a Sunday market. Los Cristianos has its own Sunday market near the harbour.

When to go & a few practicalities

Whale watching runs all year, with April to October the calmest for boats. Hiking is best from October to April when it is cooler. Mount Teide can get snow in winter, which sometimes closes the road and cable car, and it is cold at the top whatever the season, so pack a jacket even on a 25°C beach day. If you visit in February, the island's Carnival is in full swing and worth building a day around.

What is the number one thing to do in South Tenerife?

Whale and dolphin watching. Resident pilot whales and dolphins live off the south coast year-round, so sightings are close to guaranteed, and trips start from around €22. Choose a licensed "Blue Boat" operator that keeps a respectful distance from the animals.

Is Siam Park worth the money?

Yes. It is regularly rated the best water park in the world and easily fills a full day. Book online for a lower rate from about €38, arrive at opening to beat the queues, and consider a fast-pass in July and August when it gets very busy.

Do I need to book anything in advance for Mount Teide?

To stand on the actual summit peak you need a free permit reserved weeks ahead, which is separate from the cable car ticket. The cable car itself can be booked closer to the day, but it closes in high wind, so build in a spare day if the volcano is a priority.

What can I do in South Tenerife for free?

Plenty. All the beaches are free (only sunbeds cost around €10–15), you can walk the 10km seafront promenade from Los Cristianos to La Caleta, wander Adeje old town, and browse the Torviscas market on Thursdays and Saturdays. Only the parks and boat tours need paying for.
