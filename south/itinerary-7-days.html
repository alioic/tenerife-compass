7 days in South Tenerife

A week that leaves room to breathe: a beach base you barely have to leave, and three or four big days out that are worth setting an alarm for.

Home› South Tenerife› Plan Your Trip› 7-Day Itinerary

How to think about a week here

Most people who come to South Tenerife for a week are not trying to see the whole island. They want sun, a pool, a decent dinner, and a couple of days they will actually remember. This itinerary is built around that. You pick one resort, unpack once, and treat everything else as a day trip you can drive or be driven to and come home from the same evening.

The south makes that easy. Costa Adeje, Playa de las Américas, Los Cristianos and Callao Salvaje all sit within about twenty minutes of each other, and the motorway gets you to Siam Park, the whale boats and the foot of Mount Teide without much fuss. So the plan below front-loads the arrival with rest, spaces the big excursions so you are not exhausted by day four, and keeps the last day light in case your flight is early. If you have less time, there are trimmed 3 and 5 day versions further down. For help choosing a base, see where to stay.

Day 1: Arrive, settle, walk the promenade

Land, collect the hire car or transfer, check in, and resist the urge to plan anything ambitious. Get your bearings with a slow afternoon on the nearest beach, then walk the seafront promenade at golden hour. The paved coastal path runs almost unbroken from Costa Adeje down through Las Américas to Los Cristianos, and an easy stroll with a first drink somewhere along it is the right way to start. Eat close to base tonight.

Day 2: Learn your home resort on foot, sort the Ten+ card

Spend the day getting to know where you are actually staying. Find the good coffee, the supermarket, the beach with the calmest water, the bar with the sunset view. If you plan to use public buses at all this week, pick up a Ten+ card (the reloadable transport card) from a kiosk or bus station now; it is cheaper per trip than paying the driver and saves fumbling for change later. Keep the pace gentle, because tomorrow is a full one.

Day 3: Siam Park

Give the whole day to Siam Park, the Thai-themed water park above Costa Adeje that regularly gets voted the best in the world. Arrive at opening to beat the queues for the headline slides, rent a locker and a lounger early, and take a mid-afternoon break on the wave-pool beach when the crowds peak. Buy tickets ahead of time online rather than at the gate. Book this

Day 4: Whale and dolphin watching from Puerto Colón

The waters off the southwest coast hold resident pilot whales and bottlenose dolphins year-round, which is unusual and genuinely special. Head to the marina at Puerto Colón for a morning whale watching trip; smaller boats with fewer passengers give you a better, calmer experience than the big party catamarans. Morning sailings tend to have flatter seas. Back on land by early afternoon, so the rest of the day is yours for the pool. Book this

Day 5: Mount Teide and stargazing

Today you leave the coast behind. The drive up to Mount Teide, Spain's highest peak, climbs through pine forest into a lunar volcanic landscape that feels like another planet. Ride the cable car toward the summit if the weather is clear, bring a warm layer because it is genuinely cold up there, and consider staying into the evening. The national park is one of the best stargazing sites in the world, and a guided sunset-and-stars tour times it all for you. Book this

Day 6: Slow morning, then La Caleta for dinner

After Teide you have earned a lazy day. Do nothing productive: beach, pool, a spa treatment, a long lunch. Then in the evening drive the short way to La Caleta, the old fishing village on the edge of Costa Adeje, for the best seafood dinner of the trip. Book a table on the water and let the kitchen bring you whatever came in fresh. Our La Caleta dining notes point you to the tables worth the walk.

Day 7: Adeje old town or Barranco del Infierno, then home

For a last morning with a bit of substance, head inland to the old town of Adeje. It is quiet, real, and a world away from the resorts a few kilometres downhill. If you have booked ahead and your flight is late enough, the Barranco del Infierno gorge walk that starts here is a lovely final outing (numbers are capped daily, so reserve in advance). Otherwise just wander, have a coffee in the square, and head to the airport with time to spare.

Short on time?

Not everyone has a full week. Here is what to keep and what to drop if your trip is shorter.

3 days: the highlights

Do the three things you would regret missing. Day 1 for arrival and a beach afternoon, Day 2 for Siam Park, Day 3 for either whale watching or a fast Teide trip depending on what excites you more. Skip the slow days entirely and eat near your base. It is busy, but it works.

5 days: the comfortable version

Keep arrival, Siam Park, whale watching and Teide, and add one genuine rest day with the La Caleta dinner. Cut the second orientation day and the final inland morning. You get every headline experience without ever feeling rushed, which for most people is the sweet spot.

Book these before you arrive

Three things sell out or gate access, so sort them from home rather than hoping on the day:

  • Siam Park tickets online, which also skips the ticket queue at the gate.
  • Whale watching on a small-boat trip, since the good operators fill their limited seats fast.
  • Mount Teide cable car tickets, and separately the free summit permit if you intend to walk the final stretch to the crater; that permit often books out weeks ahead.

Everything else on this itinerary can be decided the night before.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a hire car for this itinerary?

Not strictly. Siam Park, whale watching and most excursions offer hotel pickup, and organised Teide tours handle the drive for you. But a car makes the Teide day, the La Caleta dinner and the Adeje morning far easier and cheaper, so most people find it worth it for a week.

When is the best time to do the Teide day?

Midweek, and check the mountain forecast the day before rather than trusting the coastal weather. Teide makes its own conditions, and cloud or high winds can close the cable car. Building it around Day 5 leaves you slack to swap it with a beach day if the forecast turns.

Is a week too long for just the south?

No. Between the beaches, the water park, the boats, the mountain and the restaurants you will not run out of things to do, and the whole point of this plan is to slow down rather than tick off the entire island. If you get restless, the north of Tenerife is a doable long day trip.

What should I book first?

Your accommodation, then in order the Teide summit permit (if you want it), whale watching, and Siam Park. The permit is the one people forget, and it is the hardest to get last minute.

Can I do this itinerary with kids?

Easily. Siam Park, the dolphin boats and the beaches are all crowd-pleasers for children, and the slow days give everyone a breather. For the Teide day, a shorter cable-car visit works better with young kids than a long summit hike.
