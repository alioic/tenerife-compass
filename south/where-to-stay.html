South Tenerife

Where to stay in South Tenerife

The resorts of the south sit almost shoulder to shoulder, but they feel very different. Pick the right one and half your holiday is sorted before you land.

Start here

The short version

Four main bases share this coast, and the borders between them blur. Costa Adeje is the polished, higher-end end. Playa de las Américas is the busy, walkable middle with the nightlife. Los Cristianos keeps a bit of the old town and gives the best value. La Caleta and El Duque are the quiet, upmarket edges.

Distances are small — you can walk the promenade from one to the next in stretches — but the atmosphere shifts a lot. Below is a quick comparison, then a closer look at each area, followed by specific hotels grouped by budget.

Area

Costa Adeje

The smart end of the coast, where most of the five-star resorts sit.

Costa Adeje runs from Torviscas and Fañabé down towards La Caleta, and takes in the glossy El Duque strip. It's clean, well kept and easy to walk, with a long promenade, designer shops around Plaza del Duque and a steady supply of good beaches. Siam Park is here too. The catch is that "Costa Adeje" covers a lot of ground — some hotels are steps from the sand, others are a real walk uphill, so always check the actual distance to the beach before booking.

Suits: couples and families who want comfort and a bit of polish, and don't mind paying for it. Also the natural home of the all-inclusive family resort.

Area

Playa de las Américas

The busy, flat, walkable middle — and the nightlife capital of the south.

Las Américas has two sides. The Verónicas area is loud, late and cheap, the classic package-holiday party zone. Head south towards Playa del Camisón and it calms down considerably, with tidy beaches and quieter hotels. The whole area is flat and very walkable, with the widest price range on the coast, so you can spend little or a lot depending on where you land.

Suits: groups and younger travellers who want bars and clubs, plus anyone who likes having restaurants, beaches and shops all within a short flat stroll. Pick the Camisón end if you want the location without the noise.

Area

Los Cristianos

The old fishing town that still feels like a town — and the best value on the coast.

Los Cristianos grew out of a fishing village and kept some of it: a genuine old quarter, a working harbour with ferries to La Gomera, and two sheltered, shallow beaches that are good for families. Prices are the most reasonable in the south, and there's plenty of everyday life alongside the tourism. Self-catering makes real sense here because you can walk to dozens of local restaurants.

Suits: families, older couples and anyone who wants character and value over a resort bubble.

Area

La Caleta & El Duque

The quiet, upmarket edges — one a former fishing hamlet, the other the glamour strip.

La Caleta was a tiny fishing hamlet and still trades on it, with the best seafood restaurants in the south lined up along the water. It's tranquil, upscale and has no nightlife to speak of. Next door, El Duque and Fañabé form the most exclusive stretch of Costa Adeje, anchored by the glamorous Playa del Duque. If you want quiet dinners and a slower pace rather than bars, this is the corner to book. Note that the quieter satellite areas like Callao Salvaje and Golf del Sur are calmer still, but a car is genuinely useful there.

Suits: couples, foodies and anyone whose idea of a good evening is a long seafood dinner by the sea, not a night out.

Booking tip

The south is cheapest in May–June and September–October, when the weather is still reliable. Peak periods — July and August, Christmas and New Year, and Carnival in February — sell out and spike in price, so book those months well ahead. Note that December to February is a second high season as northern Europeans head south for the winter sun, and the El Duque and Fañabé five-stars plus the top adults-only hotels are the first to fill. If you want one of those, don't leave it late.

Good to know

Where to stay FAQ

Which is the best area of South Tenerife to stay in?

It depends on what you're after. Costa Adeje for polish and higher-end resorts, Playa de las Américas for nightlife and a central walkable base, Los Cristianos for value and a bit of real-town character, and La Caleta or El Duque for quiet, upmarket dinners by the sea. For a first family trip, Costa Adeje and Los Cristianos are the safest bets.

Where should I stay in South Tenerife on a budget?

Los Cristianos gives you the best value on the coast, especially in self-catering apartments and aparthotels where you can walk to inexpensive local restaurants. The quieter south end of Playa de las Américas is also cheaper than the main strip. Book May–June or September–October for the lowest room rates.

Is Costa Adeje or Playa de las Américas better?

Costa Adeje is calmer, smarter and more expensive, with the cluster of five-star hotels and designer shops. Playa de las Américas is busier and more central, with the nightlife and the widest price range. If you want to party or want everything on your doorstep, choose Las Américas; if you want to relax in comfort, choose Costa Adeje.

Should I book all-inclusive or self-catering?

All-inclusive dominates the Costa Adeje family market and makes budgeting easy if you'll mostly stay put. Self-catering is the smarter pick in Los Cristianos and Las Américas, where there are dozens of restaurants within walking distance and you'd be paying for meals you don't eat. Either way, check how far your hotel actually is from the beach, as distances within "Costa Adeje" vary a lot.
