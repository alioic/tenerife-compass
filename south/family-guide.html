South Tenerife

South Tenerife with kids

Reliable sunshine, calm beaches you can actually swim at, and short drives from the airport to your door. The south of Tenerife is one of the easiest places in Europe to take young children, and this guide covers where to base yourself, which hotels earn their keep, and what to do once you are there.

Why the south works for families

The south coast is the driest, sunniest corner of the island, and that matters more with kids than it does on any other trip. You can plan a beach morning in January and expect it to happen. The days are warm without being brutal, the sea sits at a comfortable temperature for most of the year, and the clouds that gather over the north tend to stay there.

Transfers are short too. Tenerife South airport is roughly 20 to 30 minutes from the main resort towns, which is a real gift when you are travelling with a tired toddler and a mountain of luggage. Once you arrive, most family resorts are built around flat promenades, easy pushchair access, and pools designed for small children. Add a genuinely huge range of things to do, from water parks to whale watching, and you have a destination that keeps everyone happy without much effort.

Best areas for families

Costa Adeje is the polished choice. It is the newest and smartest part of the south, with wide clean beaches, a long seafront promenade that is easy to walk with a buggy, and a good spread of restaurants that welcome children. Siam Park, the island's headline water park, sits right on its doorstep. If you want comfort and don't mind paying a little more for it, this is where to look.

Los Cristianos is gentler and more down to earth. It grew out of an old fishing town, so it has a proper heart to it, and its main beach, Playa de las Vistas, is one of the most sheltered and shallow on the coast. That calm, gradually deepening water is exactly what you want with little swimmers. It is also flat, walkable, and well stocked with supermarkets and pharmacies.

One honest note. The far end of Playa de las Américas around Verónicas is the island's late-night bar strip. There is nothing wrong with the beaches nearby, but the atmosphere after dark is not aimed at families, so if you book in Las Américas, choose the Costa Adeje end rather than the Verónicas end.

Best things to do with kids

You will not run short of ideas. The south has the biggest concentration of family attractions on the island, and most are a short drive or bus ride from the main resorts.

  • Siam Park — regularly voted the best water park in the world, with slides for the brave and a gentle lazy river and wave pool for everyone else.
  • Aqualand Costa Adeje — a friendlier, smaller water park with dolphin shows and shallow areas that suit younger children.
  • Jungle Park — a hillside animal park with birds of prey displays and a good mix of exotic wildlife.
  • Monkey Park — a small, close-up zoo near Los Cristianos where kids can get near lemurs, marmosets and meerkats.
  • Camel Park — camel rides, a petting farm and a laid-back afternoon out in the hills above Los Cristianos.
  • Whale and dolphin watching — resident pilot whales and dolphins live off this coast year-round, and gentler boat trips are geared to families.
  • Submarine safari — a real submarine dive off Las Galletas, which older children tend to find genuinely thrilling.
  • Calm beaches — Playa de las Vistas in Los Cristianos and nearby Playa del Camisón are both sheltered, shallow and easy with small children.

Practical tips for a family trip

Take the sun seriously. The UV index in the south is high even when the air feels mild, and it catches families out. High-factor sunscreen, hats, and shade in the middle of the day are non-negotiable with children.

Pushchairs are easy. The Costa Adeje and Los Cristianos promenades are flat and smooth, so a buggy rolls happily along the seafront. Save the off-road carrier for hikes elsewhere on the island.

Baby supplies are covered. Large supermarkets in the resorts stock nappies, formula, baby food and everything else you might forget. No need to pack a suitcase full of it.

Self-catering versus all-inclusive. All-inclusive takes the daily thinking out of meals and works well with fussy eaters. A self-catering apartment gives you more freedom and can be cheaper for larger families who don't mind a supermarket run.

Eating out is relaxed. Restaurants here are used to children, serve early, and almost always have high chairs and simple options. Spanish families eat late, so you will often have the place to yourselves at 6pm.

Family holiday FAQ

Is South Tenerife good for a holiday with young children?

Yes. The reliable sunshine, calm and shallow beaches, short airport transfers and huge choice of family attractions make it one of the easiest European destinations for a trip with young children.

Where should we stay in South Tenerife with kids?

Costa Adeje for polished resorts and easy beaches, or Los Cristianos for a gentler, walkable town with the sheltered Playa de las Vistas. Both suit families well. If you choose Playa de las Américas, pick the Costa Adeje end rather than the busy Verónicas nightlife strip.

Which beaches are best for small children?

Playa de las Vistas in Los Cristianos and Playa del Camisón nearby are both sheltered, shallow and gently sloping, which makes them ideal for little swimmers.

What is the best water park for families?

Siam Park in Costa Adeje is the standout, with rides for older kids and a lazy river and wave pool for everyone. Aqualand Costa Adeje is smaller and better suited to younger children.

Is all-inclusive worth it for families?

It often is, especially with fussy eaters or a busy week ahead, because it removes the daily decisions around meals. Self-catering apartments can work out cheaper for larger families who are happy to cook and shop.
