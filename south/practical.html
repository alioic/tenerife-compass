South Tenerife

Plan your South Tenerife trip

The practical stuff, sorted. How to get from the airport to your resort, whether you actually need a hire car, when to visit, and a week's worth of days planned out. No fluff, just what we would tell a friend.

Getting there & airport transfers

Nearly everyone flying to the south lands at Tenerife South (TFS, also called Reina Sofía). It sits right by the TF-1 motorway, so the resorts are close.

Fly into the right airport

Tenerife South (TFS) is 15–25 minutes from the resorts. Tenerife North (TFN) is over an hour away, so choose TFS for a southern holiday.

From TFS the drive is short and simple along the TF-1. Approximate distances and times:

  • Los Cristianos — about 15–17 km, roughly 15–20 minutes
  • Playa de las Américas — about 16–18 km, roughly 15–20 minutes
  • Costa Adeje — about 18–20 km, roughly 20–25 minutes

Tenerife North (TFN) is the other airport, around 70–75 km and over an hour away by road. It mostly handles inter-island and mainland flights, so it is rarely the right choice for a southern stay. If you do land there, TITSA bus line 343 links TFN to the south.

Getting around

The resorts are compact and walkable, but a car opens up the best of the island. Here is the honest trade-off.

Hire car

You do not need a car to enjoy a beach-and-pool holiday. But if you want to reach Teide, the Masca area, quiet northern villages or the far corners of the island, a car pays for itself quickly. Public transport reaches most sights eventually, just slowly.

Rough daily rates: around €10–15 a day in low season (June), a yearly average nearer €20–25, and €30+ a day in peak periods (December, Easter and August). Book early for the busy dates.

A few things to check: pick a full-to-full fuel policy (fairest), and take the excess-waiver insurance so a scratch does not ruin your week. Local firm Cicar is well regarded. Compare prices before you commit.

The TF-1 is a free, modern, toll-free motorway and driving is on the right. Roads are good; the mountain roads up to Teide are winding but perfectly manageable. Resort parking gets tight at peak times, and blue-line zones are paid parking.

Buses (TITSA)

The green TITSA buses, known locally as guaguas, are clean, cheap and reliable. Pay the driver in cash, or tap a Ten+ (tenmás) contactless card, which costs around €2 as a one-off, can be topped up, is shareable between travellers and knocks roughly 20–30% off fares. Local hops cost about €1.45–2, and intercity trips around €3–4 at the card rate.

For Teide, line 342 runs daily from Estación Costa Adeje up to the cable car and El Portillo. It is roughly two hours each way, with just one departure up and one back per day, so plan around the timetable. Note there is no tram in the south; the tram only runs in Santa Cruz and La Laguna in the north.

Taxis

Taxis are white, metered and easy to find. Short hops around a resort cost only a few euros, which makes them handy for evenings out or when you are tired of the bus timetable.

On foot

One of the south's quiet pleasures is the flat, paved seafront promenade. It links Los Cristianos, Las Américas, Costa Adeje, Playa del Duque and La Caleta over roughly 10 km, and it is stroller- and wheelchair-friendly the whole way. You can walk between resorts for dinner without ever needing transport.

When to go & weather

The south is the sunniest, driest side of the island, with roughly 2,800+ hours of sun and only about 115 mm of rain a year. It has earned the "land of eternal spring" tag.

The busiest and most expensive spells are August, Christmas and New Year, and Easter. Winter is a second high season thanks to sun-seekers escaping the cold. For the best mix of value and weather, aim for May–June or late September–October.

Two local quirks worth knowing. The northeasterly trade winds keep the air breezy, which is why windsurfers flock to El Médano. And a few times a year a calima blows in, carrying Saharan dust: temperatures spike, the air turns hazy and it usually clears in a day or two.

Practical essentials

The small things that save you hassle once you land.

Money & cards

The currency is the euro. Cards are widely accepted, but carry some cash for buses and tips. The Canaries use IGIC (around 7%) instead of mainland VAT, so many goods are slightly cheaper here.

Tipping

Appreciated, not obligatory. Around 5–10% in restaurants, round up for coffees and beers, and €1–3 for taxis. For reference: coffee €1.50–2.50, a caña €2–3.50, a casual meal €12–20pp, a mid-range dinner with wine €25–40pp.

Plugs & power

Type C/F Europlug, 230V, 50Hz. UK visitors need a travel adapter. Same setup as most of continental Europe.

Language

Spanish is the local language, but English is widely spoken in the resorts and German is common too. A few Spanish words still go a long way.

Time zone

WET (GMT/UTC+0) in winter, WEST (GMT+1) in summer. That is the same as the UK and Portugal, and one hour behind mainland Spain.

Tap water

Safe and to EU standard, but often desalinated and not pleasant to drink, so most people buy bottled. It is fine for teeth, washing and cooking.

Safety & emergencies

Tenerife is very safe. Take the usual pickpocket care in busy spots and always heed beach flags and currents. The emergency number is 112, with English-speaking operators. Pharmacies (farmacia, green cross) are plentiful and often English-speaking, with a night rota.

Health & insurance

UK visitors should carry a GHIC and EU visitors an EHIC for state healthcare, but neither replaces travel insurance, which is strongly recommended for anything beyond basic care.

Sun & UV

UV is very high, even in winter. Use high-factor sunscreen, wear a hat, seek midday shade and drink plenty of water. The sea breeze masks how quickly you are burning.

A one-week itinerary

A relaxed week that mixes beach days with the island's best day trips. Shuffle it to suit the weather and your energy.

  1. Day 1 — Arrive and settle. Drop the bags, spend the afternoon on the beach and take an evening stroll along the promenade before dinner nearby.
  2. Day 2 — Beach and promenade day. Walk between the resorts, get your bearings and pick up a Ten+ card for the days ahead.
  3. Day 3 — Siam Park. Give the water park a full day; it is consistently rated among the best in the world.
  4. Day 4 — Whale and dolphin watching. Take a boat trip from Puerto Colón. Choose a Blue Boat operator, which follows the responsible-viewing rules.
  5. Day 5 — Mount Teide. Drive up or take line 342. Ride the cable car (book ahead; a free permit is needed for the final summit trail) and pack layers, as it is cold at altitude.
  6. Day 6 — Slow day. Beach or spa in the morning, then dinner in La Caleta as the sun goes down.
  7. Day 7 — Last morning. A final beach session, then Adeje old town or the Barranco del Infierno gorge (pre-book), before your airport transfer.

Easy swap-ins: Los Gigantes and the Masca valley, Loro Parque up north, windsurfing at El Médano, or a catamaran day trip.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a hire car in South Tenerife?

Not for a beach-and-pool holiday, as the resorts are compact and walkable with cheap buses. But if you want to reach Teide, Masca or quieter parts of the island easily, a car makes a big difference. Many visitors hire one for just two or three days.

Which airport should I fly into?

Tenerife South (TFS) for a southern holiday. It is only 15–25 minutes from the resorts. Tenerife North (TFN) is over an hour away and mostly handles inter-island and mainland flights.

When is the best time to visit?

The weather is good year-round, but May–June and late September–October offer the best balance of warmth and value. August, Christmas, New Year and Easter are the busiest and priciest.

Can I drink the tap water?

It is safe and meets EU standards, but it is often desalinated and does not taste great, so most people buy bottled water for drinking. It is perfectly fine for brushing teeth, washing and cooking.
