Siam Park: the complete guide

The Thai-themed water park in Costa Adeje that regularly gets voted the best on the planet, and how to do it without wasting money or standing in queues all day.

Home› South Tenerife› Things to Do› Siam Park
The basics

What Siam Park is, and where

Siam Park sits on the hillside above Costa Adeje in the south of Tenerife, a few minutes from Playa de las Américas. It opened in 2008 and has spent most of the years since being crowned the world's best water park in TripAdvisor's Travellers' Choice awards, which is not a title it holds lightly. The whole place is built around an elaborate Thai theme, with carved temples, a floating market and palm-shaded pools, so even the walkways are worth a look.

It is genuinely big. You can easily spend a full day here without repeating a ride, and most people do exactly that. If you are still planning your trip, it slots neatly into a stay based around Costa Adeje or Las Américas, and it pairs well with the other things to do in the south. It also happens to be one of the reasons people choose where to stay near Siam Park in the first place.

Money

Ticket options and prices

Prices below are approximate for 2026 and change with season and demand. Buying online in advance is almost always cheaper than the gate, and it saves you queueing at the ticket booths. Children under a certain height go free, and there are reduced rates for kids and Canary Islands residents.

The rides

The best rides at Siam Park

Ride

Tower of Power

The signature ride. A near-vertical 28-metre drop that fires you through a clear tube running straight through a shark and ray aquarium. Terrifying, over in seconds, and the one everyone talks about afterwards.

Ride

Singha

Billed as the world's first water rollercoaster. Two people to a raft, uphill sections powered by jets, sharp turns and real speed. Long queues for a reason, so ride it early.

Ride

The Dragon

A big rubber-ring ride into a giant tilted funnel where you swing back and forth before dropping out the bottom. Great fun in a pair and less scary than it looks.

Ride

Wave Palace

An enormous wave pool that produces some of the biggest artificial waves anywhere, up to around three metres. Sit back on the artificial beach or wade in and get thrown around.

Ride

Mai Thai River

The lazy river, and one of the longest in the world. It winds past the wave pool and even through the shark aquarium. The place to drift and recover between the bigger slides.

Ride

The Lost City

A sprawling kids' zone of bridges, towers, mini slides and water cannons. Younger children can happily disappear here for an hour while the adults take turns.

Beyond these, Kinnaree and its tilted funnel and the multi-lane racer slides are well worth your time, and Siam Beach is a white-sand lagoon with sunloungers if you just want to lie down. If a slide has a queue when you walk past, note it and come back at the hour before closing when most families have left.

Hours & seasons

Opening hours and best time to visit

The park is open every day of the year. Hours are typically 10:00 to 18:00 in the warmer months and 10:00 to 17:00 in winter, so always check the day you plan to go. Some water slides are limited or closed in cooler or windy weather, which is worth knowing if you visit outside summer.

The park is busiest from late morning to mid-afternoon in July and August. To beat the queues, arrive at opening, head straight for the biggest attractions like Singha and Tower of Power first, and save the wave pool and lazy river for the middle of the day. The last couple of hours before closing are often the quietest of all.

Getting there

The free shuttle and how to arrive

Siam Park runs a free shuttle bus that loops around the main southern resorts, with stops in Las Américas, Los Cristianos and Costa Adeje. It is free whether or not you have a ticket, and it drops you right at the entrance. Timetables are posted at the stops and on the park's site, so photograph the return times when you get off.

Driving is easy too. There is a large free car park at the entrance, and the park sits right by the TF-1 motorway at the Siam Park exit. From the airport it is roughly a 15 to 20 minute drive. Public TITSA buses also stop nearby if you prefer.

Practicalities

Food, lockers and height limits

There are several places to eat inside, from the large Thai House buffet-style restaurant to snack kiosks and ice cream stands. Prices are what you would expect from a theme park, so if you are on a budget the two-day or all-inclusive tickets start to make more sense. You cannot bring your own alcohol, though a picnic is generally tolerated.

Lockers of various sizes are available to rent for the day, and they are worth it since you will be moving around constantly. Bring or hire a towel, wear sandals you can slip off at the slide steps, and reapply sun cream often as there is a lot of open, reflective space. The big thrill rides such as Tower of Power and Singha have minimum height and age limits (around 1.4 metres), so check before promising anything to a determined child.

The verdict

Is Siam Park worth it?

Yes, for most people. It is not cheap once you add lockers and lunch, and on a peak August day you will queue. But the quality is a step above the average water park: the theming is genuinely good, the big rides deliver, and there is enough for small children and non-thrill-seekers that a mixed group can all have a good day. Go early, bring your own towel and snacks to keep costs down, and it earns its reputation. If you are travelling in shoulder season and the forecast is windy or cool, check that the slides you care about are running before you commit.

Good to know

Siam Park FAQ

How much does a Siam Park ticket cost in 2026?

A single adult day ticket starts from around €40, with children from around €28 and under-height kids usually free. Booking online in advance is cheaper than paying at the gate, and there are 2-day, Loro Parque combo and all-inclusive VIP options too.

Is the shuttle bus really free?

Yes. The Siam Park shuttle loops around Las Américas, Los Cristianos and Costa Adeje and is free to everyone, ticket or not. Timetables are posted at each stop, so note the return times when you arrive.

What is the best ride at Siam Park?

Tower of Power is the famous one, a near-vertical drop through a shark aquarium. Singha, the world's first water rollercoaster, is the other must-ride. Both draw the longest queues, so do them first thing in the morning.

How long should I spend there?

Plan for a full day. There is enough to fill 10:00 to closing, especially in summer when queues are longer. A 2-day ticket is good value if you would rather take it slowly across two visits.

Is Siam Park good for young children?

Yes. The Lost City is a large dedicated kids' zone, and there are gentle slides, the lazy river and the beach lagoon. The big thrill rides have height and age limits of around 1.4 metres, so check before queueing.
