Weather and safety

What is there to do in Tenerife when it rains?

Home› Questions› Rainy days

Short answer

First, check where the rain actually is. Tenerife's weather is local rather than island-wide, and a wet morning in Puerto de la Cruz often means brilliant sun forty minutes up the mountain. If it really is wet everywhere, Santa Cruz, La Laguna and the indoor attractions carry a day comfortably.

The trick most visitors never learn

Rain here is overwhelmingly a northern phenomenon, and it is caused by trade-wind cloud banking up against the mountains. That cloud sits in a layer. Drive up the TF-21 or TF-24 and you climb through it, and above roughly fifteen hundred metres you come out into sunshine with the cloud spread below you like a floor.

So the first move on a grey day is not to find something indoors. It is to check whether the south is clear — it very often is — or to go up. A wet day in the north is one of the best days to visit Teide, because the sea of cloud beneath the crater is exactly what people come for.

When it is genuinely wet everywhere

Santa Cruz absorbs a day easily: the Mercado de Nuestra Señora de África under cover, the TEA arts centre, the Museo de la Naturaleza y Arqueología, and Calle del Castillo for shopping. La Laguna up the tram line gives you a UNESCO old town where the tapas bars are the point and the rain is incidental.

In the north, the Lago Martiánez lido at Puerto de la Cruz is designed for days the sea is unusable, and Loro Parque has enough covered space to work. In the south, Siam Park is the counterintuitive answer — you are getting wet regardless, the queues thin out, and the park runs unless conditions are genuinely severe.

For something quieter, the Casa de los Balcones in La Orotava, the museum at the Pirámides de Güímar, and the wine museums of the north all reward an hour or two and are entirely under cover.

  • Check the south before assuming the day is lost
  • A grey north often means a spectacular Teide
  • Santa Cruz and La Laguna carry a wet day well
  • Siam Park works in the rain — you are wet anyway

How much rain are we actually talking about

Less than the question implies, in the south. The southern resorts are genuinely arid and go long stretches without measurable rain, particularly between May and September. Most visitors who ask this question are staying in the north or visiting in winter, when a wet morning that clears by lunchtime is the normal pattern rather than a washout.

The exception worth planning around is the occasional winter storm, when Atlantic swell and heavy rain arrive together. That closes the north-coast beaches and the seawater pools, and it is the one time the mountain roads are genuinely better avoided.

Also asked

Related questions

Does it rain a lot in Tenerife?

Not in the south, which is arid and often goes months with almost none. The north is considerably wetter, particularly between November and March.

Is it worth going to Teide when it is cloudy at the coast?

Often it is exactly the right day. The cloud sits in a layer below the summit, so you climb through it into sunshine with a sea of cloud beneath you.

Is Siam Park open in the rain?

It normally operates, closing only in genuinely severe conditions. A drizzly day often means shorter queues, and you are in the water regardless.
