What language do they speak in Tenerife?

Home› Questions› Language

Short answer

Spanish. Canarian Spanish has its own accent and vocabulary and sounds closer to Latin America than to mainland Spain. English is widely spoken across the southern resorts, patchily in the north, and barely at all in the small inland villages.

Canarian Spanish is its own thing

The official language is Spanish, but if you learned yours in Madrid or from a textbook, the Canarian version will take a moment. Islanders use seseo, pronouncing c and z as s, so cerveza comes out closer to servesa. Final s tends to be aspirated or dropped, which is why las casas can sound like lah casah.

Grammar differs too. Canarians use ustedes where mainland Spain uses vosotros for you plural, exactly as Latin America does. That is not a coincidence: centuries of traffic between the islands and the Americas ran in both directions, and the language went with it.

Words you will actually hear

A handful of everyday Canarian words are not the mainland ones, and knowing four or five of them makes ordering considerably easier. A bus is a guagua, not an autobús. Potatoes are papas, not patatas — which is how you get papas arrugadas, the wrinkled salt-boiled potatoes served with almost everything.

You will also meet millo for maize rather than maíz, and a friendly mi niño or mi niña used to address adults of any age, which is affectionate rather than condescending. Several of these came from Portuguese, another legacy of who was sailing past.

  • guagua — bus
  • papas — potatoes
  • millo — maize
  • mi niño / mi niña — affectionate address
  • ustedes — you plural, as in Latin America

Will English get you by?

In Costa Adeje, Playa de las Américas and Los Cristianos, comfortably. Menus are translated, hotel staff are fluent, and many businesses are run by long-settled British, German and Italian residents. You can holiday there for a week without a word of Spanish and nobody will blink.

Away from that strip it thins out quickly. In Santa Cruz and La Laguna you will find English in the tourist-facing places and less elsewhere. In the villages of Anaga, Teno and the inland north, assume none. A few words of Spanish are not just useful there — they are noticed and appreciated, because visitors so rarely bother.

One thing that is often confused: the whistled language, silbo, belongs to La Gomera, the island you can see from the south coast. It is not spoken in Tenerife.

Also asked

Related questions

Do I need to speak Spanish in Tenerife?

Not in the southern resorts, where English is spoken everywhere. Away from them it helps a great deal, and in the inland villages it is close to essential.

Is Canarian Spanish hard to understand?

It is a distinct accent — s sounds are softened or dropped and the rhythm is faster — but it is standard Spanish underneath. Learners who know Latin American Spanish usually find it easier than mainland Spanish.

What is the whistled language of the Canary Islands?

Silbo Gomero, a whistled form of Spanish used to communicate across the ravines of La Gomera. It is recognised by UNESCO and taught in schools there, but it belongs to La Gomera, not Tenerife.

Is Guanche still spoken?

No. The language of the island's pre-conquest inhabitants died out, though it survives in place names such as Taganana and Anaga and in a few everyday words.
