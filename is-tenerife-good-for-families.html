Is Tenerife good for families?

Home› Questions› Families

Short answer

Very. Short flights from most of Europe, no time difference from the UK and Portugal, warmth in every month, sheltered beaches where children can actually swim, and in Siam Park and Loro Parque two attractions good enough to build days around. The main decision is which resort, not whether.

What makes it work

The practical things first. Flights from northern Europe are four to five hours, there is no jet lag to speak of, and the climate means a February half-term feels like a summer holiday. Spain is comfortable with children in restaurants at hours that would raise eyebrows elsewhere, and highchairs, baby-changing and pharmacies are all easy to find.

Then the beaches. Playa de las Vistas and Playa del Camisón sit behind breakwaters that keep the water genuinely flat, and both have lifeguards in season. That is a meaningful difference from an open Atlantic beach, and it is why families cluster on this stretch of coast.

The two big days out

Siam Park above Costa Adeje is the strongest single attraction on the island and comfortably fills a day, with the Lost City area built for younger children and height restrictions on the bigger slides — worth checking before you promise anything. Loro Parque in the north pairs a large animal collection with a botanical garden, and a twin ticket covering both is sold at either park.

Beyond those, whale-watching trips from Los Cristianos and Puerto Colón have an unusually high success rate because the pilot whales here are resident rather than migratory, and the shorter two-hour trips suit younger children better than the full-day sails.

Choosing where to stay

Costa Adeje is the safest choice: the best-maintained beaches, the strongest hotels and a flat seafront promenade you can walk with a pushchair. It is also the most expensive part of the island.

Los Cristianos costs less for the same sunshine and has an old town with a real centre, which some families prefer to a purpose-built resort. Playa de las Américas is cheaper again and has good beaches, but choose the hotel carefully — a block near the Veronicas nightlife strip is cheap for a reason, and the difference between a quiet street and a loud one can be two hundred metres.

The north is greener and considerably cheaper, at the cost of cooler, cloudier weather and rougher sea. It suits families who want to explore more than sunbathe.

  • Sheltered beaches with lifeguards in season
  • Siam Park and Loro Parque, plus a twin ticket
  • Four to five hours from northern Europe
  • Costa Adeje for ease, Los Cristianos for value
Also asked

Related questions

Which part of Tenerife is best for families?

Costa Adeje for the best beaches and hotels, Los Cristianos for better value with a real town centre. Both have calm, lifeguarded beaches and flat seafront promenades.

Is Siam Park suitable for young children?

Yes. The Lost City area is built for them and there is a wave pool and a lazy river. Height restrictions apply on the bigger slides, so check them before the day.

Do you need a car in Tenerife with children?

Not if you are staying in the southern resorts, where the seafront is flat and walkable and buses cover the coast. A car helps if you plan to see Teide, Anaga or the north.

Is the sea warm enough for children?

It ranges from about 19°C in late winter to 23°C in early autumn. Children generally mind the cooler end less than adults do.
