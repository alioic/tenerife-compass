West Tenerife

Los Gigantes, Alcalá & Playa San Juan

Cliffs that rise 500 metres straight out of the Atlantic, fishing towns that still fish, and the only coast on the island where the sun sets over the sea in front of your table.

Get your bearings

The lie of the land

The west coast is really one long ribbon of small towns under the Los Gigantes cliff wall, about 25 to 35 minutes up the TF-47 from the southern resorts. There is no strip, no clubs, and not much that stays open past midnight. What you get instead is La Gomera on the horizon, resident pilot whales offshore, and sunsets the rest of the island drives over to watch.

Los Gigantes
Resort town

Los Gigantes

The established resort at the foot of the cliffs, with a small marina, the black-sand Playa de los Guíos beside it, and streets that climb steeply away from the water. Parking is genuinely tight. Suits couples and anyone who wants the cliff views on their doorstep.

Puerto de Santiago & Playa de la Arena
Beach base

Puerto de Santiago & Playa de la Arena

The middle stretch: an ex-fishing village with a small harbour, running into a compact resort built around a jet-black Blue Flag beach that has held its flag for over 25 years. Better hotel value than Los Gigantes itself. Suits families and beach-first trips.

Alcalá
Working village

Alcalá

A real village with a pretty plaza, cheap local cafés, rock pools to swim in, and the five-star Gran Meliá sitting quietly on its edge. Very little is staged for visitors. Suits people who want authentic and low-key, with one luxury option nearby.

Playa San Juan
Foodie town

Playa San Juan

A proper fishing town with a working harbour, a sheltered dark-sand Blue Flag beach and a long modern promenade. The restaurants here have quietly become some of the best on the coast. Suits food-led travellers and longer, slower stays.

Days out

Things to do in the west

The cliffs and the ocean do most of the work here. Boats leave from the marina all day, Masca and Punta de Teno are up the road, and in late winter the hills above Santiago del Teide turn white with almond blossom.

Whale watching
Top pick

Whale & dolphin boat trips

Pilot whales live in the channel year-round, so sightings are the norm, not the exception. Trips leave from Los Gigantes marina and run two to three hours, most with a swim stop in Masca Bay under the cliffs. Sailboat trips cost more (~€65 to €75) but carry far fewer people.

From ~€30 / person · 2–3 hours

Check boat trips
Kayak & snorkel

Kayak & snorkel under the cliffs

Paddling out from the marina puts you at the base of the 500-metre wall, which no photo from a boat deck quite matches. Tours run 1.5 to 2 hours with snorkelling gear included, and mornings are calmer than afternoons.

From ~€35 / person · 1.5–2 hours

Playa de la Arena
Free

Playa de la Arena

Small, jet-black volcanic sand, Blue Flag for more than 25 years, with sunbeds, showers and bars right behind it. It faces open Atlantic, so there is real swell some days. When the red flag is up, stay out; the currents here are not a formality.

Archipenque sunset
Free

Mirador de Archipenque

The classic cliff viewpoint above Los Gigantes, with the full wall in front of you and La Gomera behind it. Free to visit any time, and the bar-restaurant on the edge is the obvious place to sit for sunset if you can get a table.

Masca
Book ahead

Masca village & gorge

The famous ridge hamlet is a short, dramatic drive up from Santiago del Teide, mobbed at midday and lovely early or late. The gorge descent must be pre-booked (€40.66 adult, €20.33 ages 8 to 17), you arrive by bus 355 only, and it is a proper four-hour hike with mandatory boots.

Punta de Teno
Bus only

Punta de Teno

The wild north-western tip of the island: a lighthouse, lava fields and nothing else. The access road is closed to private cars every day; take bus 369 from Buenavista (€1 each way, about 20 minutes). Roadworks since January 2026 have shifted the timetable, so check it before you go.

Almond blossom
Seasonal

Almond blossom walk

From late January to early March the slopes around Santiago del Teide flower. The SL-TF 60 trail to Arguayo is about 9 km and takes 2.5 to 3 hours, through the blossom with Teide behind it. February is the reliable peak. Free, and best on a weekday.

Diving

Scuba diving

The cliff wall continues underwater, which makes this one of the better shore-adjacent dive areas on the island. Los Gigantes Diving Centre at the marina is PADI-registered and runs everything from try-dives to courses; contact them directly for current prices.

Safety note

The Crab Island tidal pool below Los Gigantes looks harmless and is not. Waves wash over it without warning and there have been fatalities, including in 2025. It is barricaded off in rough seas; only swim on genuinely calm days and always obey closures. At Playa de la Arena, treat the red flag as a hard no.

Sleep well

Where to stay in the west

Hotel prices here run noticeably below Costa Adeje for the same star rating. Most of the stock sits in Puerto de Santiago and Playa de la Arena; Los Gigantes itself is smaller and steeper, and Alcalá has exactly one big-name hotel, which happens to be one of the best on the island.

Barceló Santiago
Adults only

Barceló Santiago

Perched on the clifftop with the full Los Gigantes wall filling the view from the pool terrace. Adults-only, well run, and the sunsets from the rooms alone justify the rate.

Eat & drink

Food & drink worth crossing town for

The west eats better than its size suggests, and Playa San Juan in particular has become a small food destination in its own right. Fish comes off the boats in the harbour; book ahead for anywhere with a view.

Sunset table

Mirador Archipenque

Bar-restaurant hanging on the cliff edge above Los Gigantes. The food is decent; the view at sunset is the point. Tables on the edge are few, so book, and book early in the evening.

KV Bar Gourmet

French-influenced fusion cooking in Puerto de Santiago, strong on seafood and easily the most ambitious kitchen in the three towns. Small room, so reserve.

Rosso Pomod'Oro

The reliable Italian in Los Gigantes: proper pizzas, generous pastas, no reinvention. Exactly what you want on the second or third night when nobody can agree on anything else.

Fresh fish

Brisa Marina Taberna Marinera

On the Playa San Juan seafront, serving whatever the harbour landed. The tuna in mojo is the signature dish and the reason locals from other towns drive over.

Top rated

La Boca Rie

French cooking in Playa San Juan that has quietly collected some of the best reviews on the whole west coast. A short menu done properly. Book well ahead.

MarSalá

Seafood and Mediterranean plates in Playa San Juan, a touch more polished than the taberna up the road and a good middle option between casual and special-occasion.

Book ahead

Muelle Viejo

Canarian fish cooking with a view over Alcalá's little harbour. Small, popular with residents, and booking is essential rather than advisable.

3 Raíces

Steakhouse and wine bar in Alcalá for the night you have had enough fish. Good meat, a serious wine list, and a local crowd.

Know before you go

Practical notes for the west

Getting here is easy; a couple of the roads and car parks deserve honest warnings.

QuestionThe short answer
From the south coastTF-1 to the Chío / Guía de Isora exit, then TF-47 down to the coast. About 25 to 35 minutes from Costa Adeje, roughly 45 from Tenerife South airport.
The Masca road (TF-436)One of the most demanding roads in Europe: about 22 km, barely two cars wide in places, relentless hairpins, and coaches coming the other way. Confident drivers only, and go early before the traffic builds.
Parking in Los GigantesGenuinely tight around the marina. Use the car parks above town and walk down rather than circling the lower streets hoping.
Punta de TenoThe road is closed to private cars every day. Bus 369 from Buenavista, €1 each way, runs in regulated hours; roadworks since January 2026 mean you should check the current timetable.
SunsetsThis is the only west-facing resort coast on the island, so every evening is a show. Best spots: Mirador de Archipenque, Playa de la Arena, the Playa San Juan promenade, or an evening boat from the marina.

One planning tip

Do Masca and Punta de Teno on separate days, both in the morning. Masca fills with coach traffic by midday, and the last regulated bus back from Teno is earlier than most people expect.

Quick answers

West Tenerife FAQ

Is Los Gigantes a good base for a holiday?

Yes, if your idea of a holiday is boats, cliffs, good dinners and quiet nights. It suits couples, walkers and divers well. If you want nightlife, big shopping or long golden beaches, base yourself in the south and visit the west for a day; there is no strip here and the town is small and steep.

Can you swim at Playa de la Arena?

Yes, and it has held Blue Flag status for more than 25 years, but it faces open Atlantic and gets real swell. Swim between the flags, and stay out of the water entirely when the red flag is flying. On calm days it is a lovely, well-serviced black-sand beach.

How do I get to Punta de Teno?

By bus. The access road is closed to private cars every day, so drive or bus to Buenavista del Norte and take bus 369 (€1 each way, about 20 minutes). Roadworks since January 2026 have affected the schedule, so check the TITSA timetable on the day you plan to go.

Is the Masca road scary to drive?

It is demanding rather than dangerous if you take it seriously: about 22 km of hairpins on a road barely two cars wide, shared with coaches. Confident drivers who go early in the morning find it fine, and the views are extraordinary. If mountain roads stress you, book a tour or take bus 355 instead.

Where are the best sunsets in Tenerife?

Here. The west is the only resort coast that faces the setting sun, with La Gomera silhouetted on the horizon. The best free spot is the Mirador de Archipenque above Los Gigantes; otherwise try Playa de la Arena, the Playa San Juan promenade, or an evening sailing from the marina.
