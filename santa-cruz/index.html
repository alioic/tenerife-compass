North-east Tenerife

Santa Cruz, La Laguna & Anaga

A working capital, a UNESCO-listed university town and mountains of ancient laurel forest, all within twenty minutes of each other. This is the corner of Tenerife where the island stops performing for tourists.

Get your bearings

The lie of the land

Santa Cruz sits on the water, La Laguna sits on a plateau one tram ride uphill, and the Anaga mountains fill everything beyond, ending in villages that only got roads within living memory. Most people come up from the south for a day and wish they had allowed two.

Santa Cruz
The capital

Santa Cruz de Tenerife

A real Spanish city that happens to sit on a holiday island: plazas, museums, serious shopping on Calle Castillo, and nobody selling excursions. Come for the day, stay for dinner on Calle La Noria.

Las Teresitas
The beach

Playa de las Teresitas

The island's golden anomaly: Saharan sand shipped in by boat in the 1970s, laid under palms behind a breakwater that keeps the water flat. Ten minutes from the city, next to the fishing village of San Andrés.

La Laguna
UNESCO old town

San Cristóbal de La Laguna

The island's first capital and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, its 16th-century grid of mansions still intact. It is also a university town, so the tapas bars fill with students rather than tour groups.

Anaga
The wild bit

Anaga Rural Park

A ridge of ancient laurel forest, knife-edge hamlets and black-sand coves at the island's north-east tip, with more endemic species per square kilometre than anywhere else in Europe. Slow roads, frequent mist, worth every hairpin.

One stop worth knowing on the way up: Candelaria. Its basilica holds the Black Madonna, patron saint of the Canary Islands, and nine bronze Guanche kings stand along the seafront facing the ocean. Treat it as a 30-minute add-on rather than a destination, and it delivers.

Days out

Things to do in the north-east

Most of the best things here are free or cost pocket change, which makes a pleasant change from the excursion coast.

Las Teresitas
Free

Playa de las Teresitas

Golden imported Saharan sand, palm trees, and a breakwater that keeps the water calm enough for toddlers. Local families fill it on weekends, which tells you what you need to know. Finish with a fish lunch in San Andrés at its northern end.

Sendero de los Sentidos
Free · Easy

Sendero de los Sentidos

Short, easy loops through the laurel forest from the Cruz del Carmen visitor centre, built around what you can hear, touch and smell under the canopy. The gentlest introduction to Anaga, fine with children or without boots.

Anaga hikes
Permit for one

The longer Anaga hikes

The serious routes run 3 to 5 hours through cloud forest down to the coast. The most famous, the Bosque Encantado through El Pijaral, needs an advance permit from the Cabildo. It is free, but daily numbers are capped, so apply online early.

Guided Anaga tours

Anaga with a guide

If the hairpins or the route-finding put you off, guided hikes and minibus tours cover the highlights from Santa Cruz or the south, with someone to explain why this forest looks like nowhere else on the island.

Auditorio

Auditorio de Tenerife

Santiago Calatrava's concert hall, its white wave of a roof curling over the harbour front. Walking around the outside is free and is most of the point; interior tours run from roughly €5 to €10 if you want the engineering story.

The market
Mornings only

Mercado Nuestra Señora de África

Santa Cruz's covered market, all patios and arches: fish, cheese, flowers and everything a Canarian kitchen needs. Go in the morning; it winds down and closes around 14:00. The tapas counters inside beat any hotel breakfast.

La Laguna
UNESCO

Stroll La Laguna's old town

Wander the flat 16th-century grid, peek into mansion courtyards, and pay about €2 to climb the Concepción tower for the best view over the rooftops. Late afternoon, when the light goes gold on the facades, is the time.

Palmetum

Palmetum

One of the world's great palm collections, planted on what used to be the city rubbish tip, which makes the transformation half the story. Sea views, shade and quiet for about €6, right next to the Auditorio.

MUNA

MUNA, the archaeology museum

The Museum of Nature and Archaeology holds the island's Guanche collection, including mummies of the people who lived here before the Spanish conquest. Around €5, genuinely absorbing, and the obvious wet-weather plan.

Carnival
Book months ahead

Carnival of Santa Cruz

From February into early March the city throws one of the world's biggest carnivals, usually ranked just behind Rio: costumed parades, all-night street parties, hundreds of thousands of people. Hotels sell out months ahead, so plan in either direction.

Taganana & Benijo
The big drive

Taganana & Playa de Benijo

The hairpin road over the Anaga ridge drops through Taganana to a black-sand coast with rock stacks standing offshore at Benijo. One of the great short drives anywhere. The ocean is powerful and unguarded; swim with great caution or not at all.

Sleep well

Where to stay in the north-east

Hotels here are priced for business travellers and Spanish weekenders, not the resort market, so your money goes further than in the south. Base in Santa Cruz for museums and restaurants, or in La Laguna for atmosphere. Anaga has no conventional hotels at all, only casas rurales, small rural houses that book out well ahead.

Know before you go

Practical notes for the north-east

Getting here from the south is easy. Getting around Anaga rewards a little planning.

QuestionThe short answer
Driving from the southStraight up the TF-1 motorway, about 50 to 70 minutes to Santa Cruz depending on where you start and the traffic around the capital.
By bus from Costa AdejeTITSA express line 110 runs direct to Santa Cruz; the 111 covers the same corridor with more stops. A single is about €9.45, cheaper with a ten-journey card.
Santa Cruz to La LagunaTram Line 1 links the two in 30 to 40 minutes for €1.35. Easier than driving and parking in either city.
ParkingUnderground car parks in central Santa Cruz; in La Laguna use the car parks on the edge of the largely pedestrian old town. Las Teresitas has a big free lot behind the beach.
Driving in AnagaThe TF-12 and TF-134 are narrow, winding and slow: allow about an hour from Santa Cruz to Benijo. Weekend parking runs out by mid-morning, so go early or on a weekday. Mist on the ridge is normal, not bad luck.
What to packLa Laguna sits at about 550 metres and runs noticeably cooler than the coast, especially after dark. Bring a layer even when Santa Cruz is in shirtsleeves.

One planning tip

Do Anaga in the morning and the cities in the afternoon. The forest is clearest early, the Taganana coast is best before noon, and Santa Cruz and La Laguna only warm up socially from late afternoon anyway.

Quick answers

Santa Cruz & La Laguna FAQ

Is Santa Cruz worth visiting?

Yes, if you want the Tenerife that exists for its own residents: real plazas, museums, the market, the Auditorio and proper restaurants at city prices. It is a half-day to a day, best combined with Las Teresitas or La Laguna. Skip it only if your holiday is strictly beach and pool.

How do I get there from the south by bus?

Take the TITSA 110 express from Costa Adeje, which runs direct up the motorway to Santa Cruz, or the 111 with more stops. A single costs about €9.45 and drops if you use a ten-journey card. From Santa Cruz, tram Line 1 continues to La Laguna for €1.35.

Is Las Teresitas the best beach on Tenerife?

It is the prettiest classic beach: golden imported sand, palms, and calm water behind its breakwater, which makes it superb for families. It has fewer facilities than the southern beaches and gets very busy with local families on weekends. On a weekday morning it is hard to top.

Do I need a car for Anaga?

A car gives you the most freedom, though the roads are narrow and slow and weekend parking is scarce. Buses reach key points including Cruz del Carmen and Taganana, but frequencies are low and connections need planning. If you would rather not drive the hairpins, a guided tour covers the highlights without the stress.

When is Carnival in Santa Cruz?

The main events run from February into early March, with dates shifting each year. It ranks among the biggest carnivals in the world after Rio, and the city fills completely: closed streets, all-night parties and hotels selling out months in advance. Book far ahead, or time your trip around it if crowds are not your thing.
