What should you pack for Tenerife?

Home› Questions› Packing

Short answer

Beach clothes for the coast, and a genuine warm layer for anywhere above the resorts. The single mistake people make is packing for one climate: sea level is warm all year, Teide sits below freezing at night, and the north is several degrees cooler and considerably wetter than the south.

Layers, because this is several islands at once

A February afternoon in Costa Adeje can be shorts weather while the crater floor at Teide is below zero and Anaga is in drizzle. All three are the same day on the same island. One warm fleece or jacket and a light waterproof cover almost every scenario you will meet.

If Teide is on your list, take it seriously: hat, gloves and a proper layer are not overkill at 3,555 metres, and the cable car station is windier than the temperature suggests. If you are staying in the north, or visiting Anaga or La Laguna, add the waterproof — trade-wind cloud brings real drizzle up there most weeks of the year.

Sun, feet and plugs

At 28 degrees north the UV is high in every month, including December, and the sea breeze hides how fast you are burning. High-factor sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses are not seasonal here. Sunscreen also costs noticeably more at resort shops than at a supermarket.

Footwear catches people out twice. Black volcanic sand gets genuinely hot underfoot in the middle of the day, and many of the island's best beaches are pebbled or rocky — cheap water shoes solve both. And if you intend to walk anything at all, from the Anaga trails to Montaña Roja, bring shoes with grip; the volcanic stone is loose and unforgiving in flip-flops.

Spain uses type C and F sockets at 230 volts, so visitors from the UK and Ireland need an adapter and visitors from North America need to check their chargers handle 230V. Hotels rarely lend them out in useful numbers.

  • A warm layer and a light waterproof
  • High-factor sunscreen, hat, sunglasses
  • Water shoes for black sand and rocky beaches
  • Type C/F plug adapter, 230V

What you can leave behind

Heavy winter clothing, unless you are doing Teide at night. Formal wear, unless you have booked one of the Michelin-starred restaurants — most places, including good ones, are relaxed about dress. A hairdryer, which every hotel has. And large quantities of toiletries, since supermarkets here stock everything and cost less than the shops on the seafront.

One genuinely useful addition instead: a refillable water bottle. The tap water is safe, large supermarket bottles are cheap, and between them you avoid both buying and carrying small bottles all week.

Also asked

Related questions

Do you need a jacket in Tenerife?

Yes, at least a light one. Evenings on the coast can be cool, the north is several degrees colder than the south, and Teide is genuinely cold at any time of year.

What plug adapter do you need for Tenerife?

Spain uses type C and F sockets at 230 volts. UK and Irish visitors need an adapter; North American visitors should check their devices are rated for 230V.

Do you need walking boots in Tenerife?

Boots only for serious hikes. For most trails, including Anaga and Montaña Roja, trainers with decent grip are enough. Flip-flops are not — the volcanic stone is loose.

Is sunscreen expensive in Tenerife?

In resort shops, yes. Supermarkets are much cheaper, and buying one bottle on arrival costs less than bringing several through hand luggage restrictions.
