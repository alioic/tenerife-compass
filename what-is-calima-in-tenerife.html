What is calima in Tenerife?

Home› Questions› Calima

Short answer

Calima is a haze of fine dust and sand blown over the Canary Islands from the Sahara on an easterly wind. It turns the sky yellow-orange, pushes temperatures up sharply and cuts visibility. Most episodes are mild and pass in a day or two; severe ones are rare but can disrupt flights.

What is actually happening

Tenerife sits around three hundred kilometres off the coast of Africa. When the wind swings round to the east, it carries suspended dust from the Sahara out over the islands. The word calima simply describes that haze.

The visible effect is a milky or orange sky, a sun you can look at, and horizons that vanish — on a strong calima day you cannot see La Gomera from the south coast, and Teide disappears from the motorway. Temperatures often jump several degrees at the same time, because the air arriving is desert air.

When it happens, and how bad it gets

Calima is most common between late autumn and early spring, though it can occur in any month. Most episodes are mild: a hazy day or two, warmer than forecast, and then the trade winds resume and clear it out.

Severe events are much rarer. In February 2020 an exceptionally heavy calima cut visibility so badly that flights across the Canaries were suspended and both Tenerife airports closed for a period. That was unusual enough to make international news, which is precisely why it is worth knowing it is not the norm.

What it means for your trip

For most visitors, a mild calima means a hot hazy day and disappointing photographs. Anyone with asthma or another respiratory condition should take it more seriously: fine airborne dust aggravates those conditions, and the sensible response is to stay indoors with the windows shut on the worst days and keep any inhaler to hand.

It also means the Teide cable car view can be worthless on a calima day, since you may be looking down at haze rather than out at the islands. If you have the flexibility, that is a trip worth moving. Check the Spanish met office, AEMET, which forecasts calima episodes, and if you are flying during a heavy one, check your flight status before travelling to the airport.

  • Most common late autumn to early spring
  • Respiratory conditions need real care
  • Worth postponing a Teide trip
Also asked

Related questions

How long does calima last in Tenerife?

Most episodes last a day or two before the trade winds clear the air. Longer spells happen but are less common.

Is calima dangerous?

For most people it is uncomfortable rather than dangerous. People with asthma or other respiratory conditions should limit time outdoors during heavy episodes.

Does calima cancel flights?

Rarely. It takes an exceptionally heavy episode to reduce visibility enough to stop flights, as happened across the Canaries in February 2020. Mild calima has no effect on air travel.

Can you still swim during calima?

Yes. Calima affects the air, not the water. The main change is that it is usually hotter and the light is poor.
