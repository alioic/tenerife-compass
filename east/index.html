East Tenerife

El Médano, La Tejita & the golf coast

The wind blows, the kites fly, and nobody dresses up for dinner. This is the corner where locals keep weekend apartments, with a red volcanic cone standing over the island's best natural beaches.

Get your bearings

The lie of the land

The east is the stretch of coast around Tenerife South airport, and it splits neatly in two. El Médano and La Tejita are the natural-beach, board-sport end; Golf del Sur, Los Abrigos and Las Galletas are the quieter, greener, more grown-up end. Ten minutes inland sits Granadilla de Abona, the old municipal town, with a cobbled centre, a 17th-century convent and honest lunch menus that most visitors drive straight past on the motorway below.

Wind honesty

The trade winds define this region, and you should know that before booking. They blow most reliably from May to September and peak in July and August, which is exactly why the windsurfers and kitesurfers are here. For a lie-on-a-towel beach day, the same wind is genuinely annoying, and at La Tejita it can mean flying sand. October to December is noticeably calmer, and the sheltered corner at Playa Chica works on almost any day.

El Médano
Surf town

El Médano

A real Spanish town that happens to host a world windsurfing scene: a plaza, a boardwalk, board shops and cafés full of sandy feet. Nothing is polished and that is the appeal. Suits independent travellers, sporty types and anyone allergic to resort strips.

La Tejita & Montaña Roja
Wild beach

La Tejita & Montaña Roja

A long natural sand beach under a 171-metre red volcanic cone, all inside a protected reserve. No promenade, minimal facilities, and on windy days you will know about it. Suits beach purists who bring their own water and do not need a sunbed.

Golf del Sur & Los Abrigos
Quiet · Golf

Golf del Sur & Los Abrigos

Low-rise resorts wrapped around fairways, next to a fishing village whose harbourfront is one long row of fish restaurants. Peaceful to the point of sleepy after dark. Suits golfers, older couples and slow-dinner people.

Las Galletas & Costa del Silencio
Local · Value

Las Galletas & Costa del Silencio

A working harbour town with a mostly Spanish seafront, next to a plain apartment resort where prices stay sensible. Not pretty, but genuine, and one of the island's main diving bases. Suits divers and budget-minded longer stays.

Days out

Things to do in the east

The wind and the water carry this region. Most of the best things here are free and outdoors; the paid ones, from kite lessons to a real submarine, are the kind you remember.

Windsurf & kitesurf
Top pick

Windsurf & kitesurf lessons

This is why El Médano exists on the sporting map. Schools like the Duotone Pro Center and Kitesurf 313 teach on El Cabezo, the dedicated kite beach south of town, with steady trade winds doing half the work. Lessons start around €120 to €130 for roughly two hours; a full beginner course runs about €295 for nine hours.

The boardwalk
Free

Walk the boardwalk

The wooden walkway runs from the plaza along the beaches to El Cabezo, and it is the town's social spine. Kites in the air on one side, cafés on the other, and nobody in a hurry. Do it at the end of the afternoon when the light softens and the last riders are still out.

Montaña Roja
Free

Climb Montaña Roja

The red cone between El Médano and La Tejita is a straightforward walk to a 171-metre summit, about 30 to 40 minutes up, with both beaches laid out below you. There is no shade anywhere on it, so go early, take water, and skip the middle of the day entirely.

La Tejita
Free

La Tejita beach

Around a kilometre of natural sand with the mountain at one end and almost nothing built behind it. The stretch nearest Montaña Roja is naturist by long tradition. Facilities are minimal, so bring what you need, and on a strong wind day check the forecast before committing.

Diving

Diving at Las Galletas

Las Galletas is one of the island's main diving bases, with several centres working the reefs and volcanic seabed off this coast. Fun dives for certified divers start around €40; first-timer try-dives run roughly €50 to €90 depending on the centre and setup.

Fun dives from ~€40 · try-dives ~€50–90

Submarine Safari
Book ahead

Submarine Safari

From Marina San Miguel you can go underwater without getting wet: a real submarine dives for about 50 minutes, portholes and all. Around €57 for adults and €35 for children, and one of the few attractions here that works on the windiest day of the year.

Golf

A round on the golf coast

Two established courses sit side by side above the sea. Golf del Sur has 27 holes with green fees from about €93; Amarilla Golf starts around €79. Both prices move with the season, and winter mornings book out well ahead.

From ~€79 / round · seasonal rates

Markets
Weekly

El Médano markets

Saturday brings the artisan market to Plaza Roja, roughly 9:00 to 14:00, with local makers rather than imported tat. Wednesday morning is the farmers' market. Both are small, but they are the town at its most itself, and parking is tight while they are on.

Los Abrigos

Dinner in Los Abrigos harbour

The classic east-coast evening: arrive before sunset, walk the tiny harbour, then take a table at one of the fish restaurants lining it. The routine of picking your fish from the display and watching the light go is the whole event, and it has not changed in decades.

Sleep well

Where to stay in the east

El Médano's hotel stock is small, characterful and often booked out by returning windsurfers, so reserve early. The golf coast has the bigger resorts, and prices across the whole region undercut Costa Adeje comfortably.

The town's historic hotel, built partly on stilts over the beach, so the tide comes in under the terrace. Rooms are simple, the position is unrepeatable, and it books out fast.

Eat & drink

Food & drink worth the sandy floor

The east eats casually and eats well. Fish comes off the boats at Los Abrigos and Las Galletas, and El Médano's food scene runs from surf-shack breakfasts to one properly serious dining room.

Know before you go

Practical notes for the east

This is the easiest region on the island to reach, and the practicalities mostly come down to parking and one bus line.

QuestionThe short answer
From the airportEl Médano is about 10 minutes from Tenerife South (TFS), the shortest transfer on the island. A taxi runs roughly €12 to €16.
Getting aroundThe TF-1 motorway runs along the whole coast, so no part of the east is more than a few minutes from an exit. A car makes La Tejita and Granadilla trivial.
By busTITSA line 470 links Los Cristianos, Las Galletas, Golf del Sur, Los Abrigos and El Médano, roughly 06:00 to 23:00, every 40 to 80 minutes.
Parking at La TejitaTwo free dirt lots off the TF-643, then a 5-minute walk over the dunes. They fill on summer weekends, so arrive in the morning.
Parking in El MédanoTight on Wednesday and Saturday mornings when the markets are on. Outside those windows it is manageable.
Quick answers

East Tenerife FAQ

Is El Médano good for a holiday?

Yes, if you want a real town rather than a resort: a plaza, a boardwalk, natural beaches and a laid-back, sporty crowd. It suits independent travellers, wind-sport people and anyone bored of hotel strips. If you want big pools, nightlife and guaranteed still air on the sunbed, the southern resorts fit better.

Is it too windy to sunbathe?

Sometimes, honestly. The trade winds blow most reliably from May to September and peak in July and August, and at La Tejita a strong day means flying sand. October to December is calmer, and Playa Chica is the sheltered corner that works on almost any day. Check the wind forecast and plan beach days around it.

Can beginners learn to kitesurf in El Médano?

Yes, this is one of the best places in Europe to start. Schools such as the Duotone Pro Center and Kitesurf 313 teach beginners on El Cabezo, the dedicated kite beach. Expect around €120 to €130 for a roughly two-hour lesson, or about €295 for a full nine-hour beginner course.

Is La Tejita a nudist beach?

Partly. The end of the beach nearest Montaña Roja is naturist by long tradition, while the rest is mixed and textile. Nobody polices a boundary; the transition is gradual and everyone coexists without fuss. Facilities are minimal along the whole beach, so bring water and shade whichever end you choose.

How close is the airport to El Médano?

About 10 minutes by road from Tenerife South (TFS), with a taxi costing roughly €12 to €16. It is the shortest airport transfer on the island, which makes the east ideal for short breaks and for the first or last night of a longer trip. Flight noise in town is occasional rather than constant.
