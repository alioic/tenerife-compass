Can you drink the tap water in Tenerife?

Home› Questions› Tap water

Short answer

Yes. Tap water in Tenerife is treated to EU drinking-water standards and is safe to drink. Most residents and visitors still buy bottled water, because the taste — a legacy of desalination and the island's volcanic geology — puts a lot of people off. It is a preference, not a health warning.

Safe, but not to everyone's taste

Spain applies EU drinking-water rules, and Tenerife's supply is treated and monitored to meet them. Brushing your teeth, filling a glass, cooking with it and making coffee are all completely normal here, and nobody boils water before use.

What people object to is flavour. Much of the island's water is either desalinated seawater or comes up through volcanic rock via galerías, the tunnels bored horizontally into the mountains to reach groundwater. Both routes leave mineral characteristics that many describe as flat, faintly salty or slightly metallic. Coffee made with it tastes different from what you are used to.

Why hotels often say otherwise

You will see signs in some hotel bathrooms advising guests not to drink from the tap. This is usually caution rather than a statement about the municipal supply: hotels cannot vouch for their own internal plumbing, storage tanks and pipework, and a blanket sign is simpler than explaining the distinction.

Water quality also varies by municipality, since supply and treatment are handled locally rather than island-wide. Water in one town can taste noticeably different from water twenty minutes away.

Ice, salad and coffee

The follow-up question is usually about everything made with water rather than the water itself. Ice in bars and restaurants, salad washed in the kitchen, coffee, and juice made up from concentrate are all fine, because they all start from the same supply that meets the same standards.

This is worth saying plainly because travellers arriving from countries where tap water genuinely is a risk often carry the habits with them — refusing ice, avoiding salad. None of that is necessary in Tenerife, and it will make eating out unnecessarily awkward.

What most people actually do

Buy the large five- or eight-litre bottles from a supermarket rather than small ones from a beach kiosk. The price difference is substantial and it cuts the plastic considerably. A refillable bottle plus one big supermarket bottle covers a family for days.

If you would rather not buy water at all, a filter jug or filter bottle deals with the taste without dealing with a health problem, because there was not one to begin with. Restaurants will bring bottled water by default; if you want tap, ask for agua del grifo, and be aware that some places will politely tell you they only serve bottled.

  • Taste is the objection, not safety
  • Large supermarket bottles are far cheaper

Also asked

Related questions

Can you brush your teeth with tap water in Tenerife?

Yes, without any concern. The water meets EU drinking-water standards.

Why does Tenerife tap water taste salty?

Much of the supply is desalinated seawater or groundwater drawn through volcanic rock, and both leave a mineral character that many people find flat or faintly salty.

Is bottled water expensive in Tenerife?

Not from supermarkets, where large bottles cost very little. Small bottles from beach kiosks and hotel minibars cost many times more.
