The best time to visit Tenerife

There is no bad month on the south coast, but May, June, late September and October give you the best mix of warm sea, comfortable heat, thinner crowds and prices that have not been marked up.

Home› Best time to visit

The short answer

Go in May, June, late September or October. Daytime highs sit between 24°C and 28°C, the sea is at its warmest in September at 24°C, and you are outside the three periods when the island fills up and hotels charge whatever they like: August, Christmas to New Year, and Easter. If you only care about escaping a northern winter, January to March still delivers 22–23°C and sunshine, but it is a proper high season here, not a bargain.

Tenerife does not really have a weather-based off season. The south coast gets around 2,800 hours of sun a year and only about 115 mm of rain, and almost all of that rain falls on a handful of winter days. What changes month to month is not whether the sun shows up. It is how hot it gets, how warm the water is, how many people are sharing the beach with you, and what a room costs.

That last part surprises people. Tenerife has two high seasons rather than one. Summer brings the families and the Spanish holidays; winter brings northern Europeans chasing 22°C in February. December is one of the most expensive months of the year here. The genuinely quiet, genuinely good-value windows are narrow, and they sit either side of summer.

Cost and crowds

When it is cheapest, and when it is packed

The cheapest months

Late April to June and late September through November are where the value sits. Flights and hotels drop noticeably once Easter is over and again once the summer holidays end, and the weather in both windows is arguably better than in the peaks. November is the quietest month of all before the winter-sun bookings kick in around the start of December.

Do not assume winter is cheap simply because it is winter. Northern Europeans book Tenerife in January and February precisely to escape their own weather, which keeps demand and prices high through to March. Our where to stay guide is worth reading before you book, because the price gap between resorts in the same month is often bigger than the gap between seasons.

The busiest months

Three periods stand apart: August, Christmas to New Year and Easter. In August the south coast is at capacity, sunbeds go early, restaurants need booking and hire cars run out. The Christmas and Easter peaks are shorter but just as expensive. If your dates are fixed to one of these, book months ahead rather than hoping for a late deal.

The catch nobody mentions

North and south are not the same holiday

Every temperature in the table above is a south-coast number. The north of the island runs 2–4°C cooler and carries a real chance of morning cloud in any month of the year. Locals call the low grey layer that sits over Puerto de la Cruz the panza de burro, the donkey's belly. It is not a flaw in your holiday: that cloud is exactly why the north is green, why the banana plantations are there, and why the food and the towns are more interesting on that side.

The practical consequence is that "the best time to visit Tenerife" depends on where you sleep. If you want guaranteed poolside sun in February, stay south. If you are heading north, expect a grey start to some mornings that usually burns off by lunchtime, and pack a jacket for the evening even in July.

Wind and calima

Persistent north-east trade winds blow across the island for most of the year. On the south coast they take the edge off summer heat, and at El Médano they are the entire point: it is one of Europe's better windsurfing and kitesurfing spots because of them. On an exposed beach in May the breeze can make 24°C feel cooler than it reads.

A few times a year the wind flips and a calima arrives, pushing hot dusty air off the Sahara. Temperatures can jump into the high 30s, the sky turns hazy and yellowish, visibility drops and air quality gets poor for a day or two. It is uncomfortable rather than dangerous for most people, and it is usually short-lived. There is no season you can book around to avoid it, so treat it as bad luck rather than a planning factor.

Sea temperature

The Atlantic here is always cooler than the air, thanks to the currents running past the islands. The range is narrow: 19°C at its coldest in February, 24°C at its warmest in September. Plenty of people swim year-round, but if you want water that feels genuinely warm rather than refreshing, aim for August to October. Good to know

Best time to visit FAQ

What is the cheapest month to visit Tenerife?

May, June and November are consistently the best value. They fall between the two high seasons, so flights and hotels are cheaper while daytime highs are still 24–26°C. August, Christmas to New Year and Easter are the most expensive periods by a wide margin.

What is the hottest month in Tenerife?

August, with average highs around 29°C on the south coast. It is also the busiest and priciest month. July and September are close behind at 28°C, and September has the warmer sea.

When is the sea warmest in Tenerife?

September, at around 24°C. The sea lags behind the air, so it is coolest in February at 19°C and warmest at the end of summer. It is always cooler than the air temperature because of the Atlantic currents.

Is Tenerife worth visiting in winter?

Yes, and that is why winter is a second high season here. Highs of 22–23°C from November to March with very little rain make it one of the few places in Europe with dependable winter sun. Just do not expect low-season prices, especially over Christmas.

What is a calima and when does it happen?

A calima is hot, dusty air blown over from the Sahara. It happens a few times a year with no fixed season. Temperatures can jump into the high 30s, the sky turns hazy and air quality drops for a day or two before conditions return to normal.
