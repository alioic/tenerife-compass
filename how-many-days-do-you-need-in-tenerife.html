Choosing your trip

How many days do you need in Tenerife?

Home› Questions› How many days

Short answer

Seven is the sweet spot. It gives you a base on one coast plus Teide, Anaga and the west without living in the car. Three or four days works if you accept seeing one corner. Ten to fourteen lets you split between north and south, or add La Gomera.

Three or four days

Enough for one region and one big day out, and no more. Base yourself in the south, spend the days on the beach and give one of them to either Teide or a boat trip from Los Gigantes. Trying to add Anaga as well means most of a short trip spent driving.

This length suits a winter-sun break where the point is warmth rather than sightseeing. It is also the right length for a first look if you suspect you will come back.

A week — the one most people should book

Seven days is where the island opens up. A workable shape: two or three days settling into a southern base with beaches and the promenade, one full day at Teide, one day north to Puerto de la Cruz or La Orotava, one day in Anaga or at La Laguna and Santa Cruz, and one for the west — Los Gigantes, Masca and the sunset coast.

That leaves the island properly seen without any day feeling rushed, and it is why the seven-day itinerary is the shape most visitors settle on. Add a hire car for at least three of those days; the rest you can do on foot and by bus.

Ten to fourteen days

At ten days the honest recommendation changes: split your stay. Four or five nights in the north around Puerto de la Cruz and the rest in the south gives you two genuinely different holidays, and it removes the long drives that otherwise eat the middle of the trip.

Two weeks also makes La Gomera realistic as an overnight rather than a rushed day trip, and gives room for the walking that Anaga and Teno deserve. Beyond a fortnight, most visitors are repeating themselves unless they are here to walk seriously or to do nothing at all — both of which Tenerife rewards.

One caveat on all of this: distances mislead. The island is only about eighty kilometres across, but mountain roads mean an hour and a half between the north and south coasts is normal. Plan by driving time, not by the map.

  • 3–4 days: one region, one big day out
  • 7 days: the whole island without rushing
  • 10–14 days: split north and south
  • Plan by driving time, not distance
Also asked

Related questions

Is 5 days enough for Tenerife?

Comfortably enough for a southern base plus Teide and one other big day, such as the west coast or Anaga. You will not see the whole island, but nothing will feel rushed.

Do you need a car for a week in Tenerife?

For at least part of it. Three days of hire covers Teide, the north and the west; the rest of the week works fine on foot and by bus if you are based in the south.

Can you see the whole of Tenerife in a week?

The main regions, yes, at a sensible pace. What a week does not give you is time to walk Anaga and Teno properly, or to add another island.
