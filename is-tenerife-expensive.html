Costs and practicalities

Is Tenerife expensive?

Home› Questions› Costs

Short answer

By European standards, no. The Canaries have lower sales tax than mainland Spain, and eating and drinking away from the seafront is inexpensive. But the gap between resorts is large: the same day costs far more in Costa Adeje than in Los Cristianos or the north, and almost all of the difference is under your control.

Where Tenerife is genuinely cheap

The Canary Islands do not use mainland Spain's VAT. They apply their own lower indirect tax, which is one reason alcohol, tobacco, fuel and electronics are noticeably cheaper here than on the mainland or in northern Europe.

Public transport is the other bargain. TITSA buses cover the island for a few euros a journey, and the tram between Santa Cruz and La Laguna costs well under two euros. Beaches are free, most viewpoints are free, and the walking — Anaga, the Teide crater floor, the coastal promenades — costs nothing at all.

Where it is not

Seafront restaurants in Costa Adeje and Playa de las Américas charge resort prices, and the mark-up over a place one street back is often startling for identical food. Sunbed and parasol hire at the smartest beaches runs to the higher end of ten to fifteen euros a day, while quieter beaches charge noticeably less and many people just bring a towel.

Attractions are the real budget item. Siam Park costs from around forty euros for an adult day ticket, with a twin ticket combining it with Loro Parque at roughly seventy. Whale-watching trips start from about thirty euros. Two attraction days for a family adds up faster than a week of meals.

The four things that move your budget most

Choosing the resort does most of the work. Costa Adeje is the island's most expensive base and Los Cristianos, twenty minutes south, is meaningfully cheaper for the same sunshine. The north — Puerto de la Cruz and around — is cheaper again, at the cost of cooler and cloudier weather.

After that it is habits rather than luck. Eat where the menu is not translated into five languages. Look for the menú del día at lunch, which is the cheapest proper meal of the day almost anywhere in Spain. Buy water and breakfast things from a supermarket rather than a kiosk. And be honest about whether you need a hire car, because if you are staying put in the south you probably do not.

  • Pick the resort deliberately — it is the biggest lever
  • Eat one street back from the seafront
  • Menú del día at lunch, not dinner
  • Skip the car if you are not leaving the resort
Also asked

Related questions

Is Tenerife cheaper than mainland Spain?

For alcohol, tobacco, fuel and electronics, yes, because the Canaries apply their own lower indirect tax rather than mainland VAT. Restaurant and hotel prices in the big resorts are comparable to or above the mainland.

What is a menú del día?

A fixed-price lunch — usually a starter, a main, dessert or coffee, and a drink. It is the cheapest way to eat a proper meal in Spain and is served at lunchtime on weekdays.

Which part of Tenerife is cheapest?

The north, around Puerto de la Cruz, and in the south Los Cristianos rather than Costa Adeje. Costa Adeje is the most expensive base on the island.
