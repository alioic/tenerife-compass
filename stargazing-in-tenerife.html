Where can you see the stars in Tenerife?

Home› Questions› Stargazing

Short answer

Teide National Park, and it is genuinely world class. High altitude, dry air and a national law limiting light pollution give Tenerife some of the clearest skies on Earth — good enough that professional observatories are sited here. You can drive up yourself for free, or join a guided tour with telescopes.

Why the skies here are so good

Three things combine. The park sits above two thousand metres, which puts you above the layer of cloud that the trade winds pile against the mountains — the sea of cloud you look down on from the road. The air above it is unusually dry and stable. And since 1988 Spanish law has restricted lighting and air traffic around the observatories to protect the sky itself.

That is not a tourist claim. The Teide Observatory at Izaña is a working professional facility used by institutions from several countries, and the island is designated a Starlight Reserve. On a moonless night at altitude the Milky Way is bright enough to cast a sense of depth you will not have seen from anywhere in northern Europe.

Going up on your own

It costs nothing. Drive up the TF-21 from Vilaflor or La Orotava, or the TF-38, and stop at one of the miradors on the crater floor. The Roques de García car park and the Llano de Ucanca are popular. Bring far more clothing than feels reasonable: at that altitude it drops below freezing at night, and the wind makes it worse.

Two cautions. The mountain roads are winding and unlit, and driving them tired after a long night is where the risk actually lies — not in the stargazing. And avoid the few nights either side of a full moon, which washes out everything faint. Check the moon phase before you commit to a date.

Or booking a tour

Guided trips leave from the southern resorts most evenings and typically include transport, telescopes, a guide who can actually find things, and often dinner. Sunset-and-stars combinations, which take you up for the sunset above the clouds and stay for the dark, are the most popular format.

The case for a tour is straightforward: someone else drives the mountain road in the dark, the telescopes are better than anything you brought, and you get told what you are looking at. The case for going alone is that it is free and you can stay as long as you like. Both are good nights.

Note that the cable car does not operate at night, so evening trips are about the crater floor rather than the summit.

  • Free to drive up and look
  • Dress for below freezing, whatever the coast is doing
  • Avoid the nights around a full moon
  • Tours handle the driving and bring telescopes

Related questions

Do you need a telescope to see the Milky Way in Tenerife?

No. On a clear moonless night at altitude in Teide National Park the Milky Way is easily visible to the naked eye. A telescope adds planets and deep-sky objects.

Can you visit the Teide Observatory?

It is a working professional facility, not a public attraction, though guided daytime visits are offered at certain times. Check the observatory's own site for current arrangements before planning around it.

Is stargazing in Tenerife cold?

Yes, genuinely. The crater floor sits above two thousand metres and drops below freezing at night even when the coast is warm. Bring proper layers, hat and gloves.

What is the best time of year for stargazing in Tenerife?

Any month can be excellent, since it depends on the night. Pick a date away from the full moon and check the forecast for calima, which hazes the sky.
