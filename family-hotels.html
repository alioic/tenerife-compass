The best family hotels in Tenerife

With kids, four boring things matter more than the brochure shots: how much water there is, how much shade, how far you really have to walk to the beach, and whether there's something for a three-year-old and a fourteen-year-old on the same afternoon.

What this list is

Every hotel below is a real, operating property, listed with the star rating it actually holds.

Most family hotels here sit in the south, for good reason: it's the sunniest corner of the island and where the big resorts were built. Los Cristianos has the most sheltered swimming at Playa de las Vistas, Costa Adeje has the five-star cluster and Siam Park, and the west and south-east give you space if you don't mind driving.

How we picked these

Star ratings are verified against each hotel's own listing. We only mention kids facilities we could confirm; where a detail was unclear we left it out rather than guess. Prices are approximate starting rates in shoulder season and climb sharply in July, August and at Christmas. Every hotel has a trade-off, and we say what it is.

Before you book

What to look for in a family hotel here

Six things that matter more than the star rating.

Shade around the pool. The sun is relentless from about eleven to four, and a pool deck with no cover is a real problem with small children. Look at photos of the pool area, not the lobby.

Kids club age ranges. "Kids club" can mean anything. Iberostar's Star Camp splits into bands from babies up to 17, and Hard Rock runs separate clubs for under-3s, primary-age kids and teens. Others take 4 to 12 and nothing more. If you have a 14-year-old, ask before you book.

The walk to the beach. The most misread thing about Costa Adeje. Some hotels open onto the sand, others are a fifteen-minute uphill trudge back with a buggy and wet towels. Check the map before paying.

All-inclusive or self-catering. All-inclusive helps with young kids: no negotiating over lunch, no hunting for a high chair, no bill anxiety when a toddler eats three bites. Self-catering wins for longer stays, for babies on bottles, and in Los Cristianos where cheap restaurants are a short walk away.

Room type and floor. Ask for a ground-floor or pool-view room: you can watch the pool from the balcony while a baby naps, and skip the lift queue with a buggy. Read the square metres, not the label.

Cots and extras. Most hotels provide a cot free or for a small charge, but they run out in peak season. Request it when you book, not on arrival, and do the same for a high chair or an in-room fridge.

Location

Where to base yourself

The hotel matters less than the streets around it.

For a first family trip the south is the straightforward choice. Los Cristianos has the calmest beach on the island, a real town with ordinary restaurants, and the best prices. Costa Adeje is smarter, with the five-star cluster and Siam Park, but distances to the sand vary wildly, so verify yours. Full breakdown in where to stay in the south, plus South Tenerife with kids.

The west trades some sunshine for cliffs, fewer crowds and the whale-watching boats. The north is greener and cheaper but cloudier, with a rougher sea, so it suits older children more than a beach fortnight with a three-year-old. Many people base in the south and drive north for a day.

FAQ

Common questions

Which part of Tenerife is best for families?

The south. It's the sunniest part of the island and where the family resorts, the calm beaches and Siam Park are. Los Cristianos has the most sheltered swimming and the best prices; Costa Adeje has the bigger, higher-end resorts. The north is beautiful but cloudier with a rougher sea, so it works better as a day trip than a base with small children.

Is all-inclusive worth it with kids in Tenerife?

With children under about eight, usually yes: it removes the daily argument about lunch and stops the bill creeping up every time someone wants an ice cream. Once your kids are older, or if you're in Los Cristianos where cheap restaurants are a short walk away, self-catering often works out better.

Which hotel is best for toddlers?

GF Gran Costa Adeje is the strongest all-rounder, with a splash pad, a Mini Club and the indoor Magic Den for the hours when the sun is too strong. Iberostar Selection Anthelia is the other obvious choice, with children's pools, slides, a pirate ship and a kids club that takes babies.

Where should we stay for Siam Park?

Siam Park is in Costa Adeje, so anywhere in Costa Adeje puts you within a short taxi ride. If you plan to go more than once, HOVIMA Jardín Caleta is roughly 400 m away, so you can walk over in the morning and come back at lunchtime with tired children.

Do family hotels in Tenerife provide cots?

Most do, free or for a small nightly charge, but stock is limited and they run out in July and August. Request one when you book rather than when you arrive, and do the same for a high chair or an in-room fridge. Confirm directly with the hotel, since policies differ even within a chain.
